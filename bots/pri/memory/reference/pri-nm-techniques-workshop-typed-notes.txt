                     Right Sidelying Left Adductor Pull Back with Knee to Knee



Reference: L Post Calcaneus/Heel (Low)


Referent(s): L IOs/TAs (High), R Med Long Arch (Low)


Cross-Referencing: L adductor with sense of L posterior ground/floor; L adductor with R
evertors; L quads and L hamstrings and L adductors with R hip IR’s (R TFL, R glute med/min, R
adductor magnus)


What are we inhibiting?
    1) Right thoracic diaphragm – by empowering the left
    2) L post. outlet, L ant. inlet, R post. inlet, R ant. Outlet
    3) L posterior ground with R forward movement
    4) L rear foot with L abs and R forefoot (end of double LE stance as body is about to shift to
       the left; left heel is about to move up as right toe comes off floor for swing)

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Individuals having difficulty rotating upper trunk to right as lower trunk is positioned to
       the left
    2) Individuals who need sense of “load” on the left to sense right upper arm/right chest wall
       ‘freedom’. In other words, those who remain ‘static’, overtight, on the right.

Other notes:
Pushing left hand down into the floor engages R TR with L AF IR (L IOs/TAs).
Helps sense of moving in a leftward retro direction.
Helps individuals learn how to “lower” upper body to lower body (floor) in a horizontal position
before going vertical.
Also inhibits L short FA ER’s, R short AF IR’s, and R short back rotators (R QL and R IO/TAs).

Left Sidelying Foot Toward Ceiling with Resisted Right Glute Max and Right Apical Expansion



Reference: L Ischial Seat (Mod), R Med Long Arch (Low)


Referent(s): R Lat Post Upper Chest (R LT/Triceps) (Mod), L Ant AF Capsule (L Adductor)
(High), L IOs/TAs (High), R Post Hip (R Glute Max) (Mod)


Cross-Referencing: Sense of R LE pronation with L thoracic lateral flexion and L LE adduction
*Ron originally started reading the wrong page, so please use this vs what he started reading.


What are we inhibiting?
    1) Overuse of R IOs/TAs/EOs and R Rectus Abdominis
    2) R BC
    3) R hip flexor from both psoas and TFL
    4) TF ER’s
    5) R intercostals

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
Those who need:
    1) Sense of the ‘left’, sense of ‘shift’ to the left
    2) Spinal stability to reduce R thoracic spinal curvature
    3) Relief from sitting at a computer with right hand mouse overwork
    4) R shoulder pain/impingement
    5) Interscap/infrascap pain and have flat blacks
    6) Limited head/cervical lateral flexion to the right (L OA compression/R OA
       decompression)
    7) Following R subclavius release

Other notes:
L HG ER (teres minor/infraspinatus) retracts the infraspinatus fossa and assists with posterior
mediastinal expansion as the left arm ER’s, which assists in forcing R apical chest wall upwards
(R apical expansion).
L HG ER also promotes L IR of ribs, which provides cross referencing of anterior rib
stabilization (abs) with posterior stabilization from teres major/infraspinaus on one side of
scapula with SA on other.

                                         Stair Short Seated Balloon



Reference: L Ischial Seat (Mod)


Referent(s): L IOs/TAs (High), L Post Calcaneal Tuberosity (Low), R Med Long Arch (Low), L
Post Mediastinum (High), R Lateral Post Upper Chest (Mod)


Cross-Referencing: L dorsiflexors with sense of L innominate posterior rotation;
R foot evertors, L tibial pronators, and R FA ER’s with L posterior mediastinal expansion


What are we inhibiting?
    1) R hemilateral sense of foot ground, R ischial ground and R ZOA ‘grounding’
    2) Latissimus, longissimus, interscap muscles
    3) Quadriceps

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Individuals who are unsteady on their left foot and cannot “pause” without leaning back
       and to the right.
    2) Those who walk with a wide base of gait.
    3) Those who cannot squat secondary to short quads and hip flexors.

Other notes:
A good technique to prepare one for retro activity and cranio activity.
Best position/technique to establish, maintain, experience sense, and facilitate posterior
mediastinums AND upper lateral chest wall expansion upon both inhalation AND exhalation.

                                         Long Seated Press Down



Reference: R Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Tricep) (Mod) R>L,
L Posterior Calcaneus/Heel (Low) L>R


Referent(s): L IOs/TAs (High) L>R


Cross-Referencing: L heel with R heel; L IOs/TAs with R IOs/TAs; R LT/Tricep with L
LT/Tricep; L ischial tuberosity with R ischial tuberosity; L ischial tuberosity with R Lateral
Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Tricep) and VICE VERSA


What are we inhibiting?
    1) Flexors of the hip and neck through cross referencing of hamstrings with triceps

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Individuals with protracted shoulders and deep lumbar curvatures
    2) Periscapular weakness
    3) Bicipital tendonitis
    4) Those who overwork with their abdominal rectus and anterior chest and neck convergent
       pullers (SITTERS!)

Other notes:
This technique establishes a good awareness of 90° SLR with neutral thoracic orientation, so one
can balance thoracic scapula stability (mid/lower trap, SA, rhomboids, subscap) with tricep
function.
Enhances subscap/SA co-activation with abdominals and dorsiflexion.

        Seated Supported Respiratory Left AF IR/FA IR with Intercostal Pulley Inhibition



Reference: L Ischial Tuberosity (Mod)


Referent(s): R Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Tricep) (Mod), L Anterior AF Capsule (L
Adductor) (High), L IOs/TAs (High), R Med Long Arch (Low)


Cross-Referencing: 1) COM to L with weight/compression at L BC, L thorax-abdominals;
2) L hip and top of chair and R arch to floor as L abs engage


What are we inhibiting?
    1) R IOs/TAs
    2) R upper traps
    3) R scalenes/BC muscles
    4) R intercostals
    5) L TFL
    6) R ant. outlet, L post. outlet, L ant. inlet, R post. inlet
    7) R posterior mediastinum
    8) L levator scapulae

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Individuals with poor sense and strength of reaching overhead with right arm, in sitting or
       standing.
    2) Individuals who can’t reach up and out with one hand as they turn upper body to the
       same side.

Other notes:
Another reason to use this technique is to sense L adductors and L glute med/min activity during
L posterior mediastinum and R upper lateral/apical expansion.
*A cortical mindful, physical performance.
*Pelvic/hip anchor for contralateral release/flow.

                90-90 Supported Wall Leg Lift with Alternating Resisted Arm Reach
 (*Label the bottom left picture ‘A’ and the bottom right picture ‘B’ for the corresponding notes below.)

Reference: A) L Posterior Calcaneal Tuberosity/Heel (Low)
           B) R Posterior Calcaneal Tuberosity/Heel (Low)


Referent(s): A) R Posterior Hip (Glute Max) (Mod), L IOs/TAs (High), L Posterior
                Mediastinum (High)
             B) L Posterior Hip (Glute Max) (Mod), R Lat Post Upper Chest (R LT/Tricep)
                (Mod), L Posterior Mediastinum (High)


Cross-Referencing: A) L adductor region & R gluteal region & L anterior hip & L posterior
                      mediastinum
                   B) R adductor region & L gluteal region & R low trap/tricep


What are we inhibiting?
    A) L AIC/R BC pattern
    B) L low back extension/lordosis and L low trap/triceps

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Individuals with “dissociation” issues or syndromes.
    2) Those with poor sense coefficient friction of ground and awareness of ipsilateral

*This alternating technique secures the lower extremity that is responsible for ipsilateral lower

                    Right Sidelying Left Anterior Glute Med with TFL Inhibition


Reference: L Ischial Tuberosity (Mod)


Referent(s): L IOs/TAs (High), R Posterior Hip (Glute Max) (Mod)


Cross-Referencing: L posterior thigh engage with R outside hip/thigh and L hip IR’s


What are we inhibiting?
    1) L TFL!
    2) R hip adductors and L rectus femoris

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Individuals who have poor single leg strength when on L leg with step overs, step
       throughs, etc.
    2) Those who have flat left feet – low left foot arches, over pronate and don’t know how to
       pronate.

Other notes:
Could use a ball between the knees to get more anterior glute med/min vs. abductor fiber.
Could put a bolster under right ankle for more R FA ER vs. R FA abduction.
Could place light weight on L ankle to increase L glute strength.
*Good orthopedic way to isolate L hip IR’s using R hip as pivot point and gravitational influence
on L ankle.
*Could extend back and hip as tolerated which would emulate prone hip isolation at cost of
inhibiting TFL’s.

                                  Left Sidelying Supported Trunk Lift


Reference: L IOs/TAs (High), L Posterior Calcaneal Tuberosity/Heel (Low)


Referent(s): R Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Triceps)


Cross-Referencing: Should feel L abs engage with L plantar/heel force, L neck (SCM), superior
obliques of extraocular muscles, R low trap/triceps


What are we inhibiting?
    1) R calcaneus
    2) R ischial seat (adductor)
    3) L canines and R molars
    4) L posterior hip (glutes)
    5) L posterior upper chest or L low trap/triceps (along with R bicep/R pec)
    6) R anterior AF capsule
    7) R IOs/TAs

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Vestibular patients who have difficulty negotiating leftward figure 8 walking while
       looking straight ahead.
    2) Superior T4 patients
    3) L thoracic diaphragm weakness/SOB
    4) Clenchers
    5) Those who just received a PRI MMOO or MOOO orthotic

Other notes:
Pillow under pelvis aligns pelvis bowl with cranial bowl, while inferior pelvis is leftward as
craniums moves rightward through L SCM and R LT/Tricep.
Wall keeps feet anchored, and allows axis of torso-pelvis-cranium to rotate.

             B) L IOs/TAs (High), R Med Long Arch (Low)

In picture A, emphasize inhalation, and in picture B, emphasize exhalation.

                            Single Leg Stance with Contralateral Glute Max
        (*Label the top picture ‘A’ and the bottom picture ‘B’ for the corresponding notes below.)

Reference: A) L Calcaneus (Low), R Posterior Hip (Glute Max) (Mod)
           B) R Med Long Arch (Low)


Referent(s): A) L IOs/TAs (High) during right leg pull back with R FA ER
             B) L Glute Max, L Ischial Tuberosoity (Mod)


Cross-Referencing:
A) L glute max with R glute max (L glute max and med and L abs may feel working harder than
R glute max). This is ok as long as one can sense R FA IR for activation of L medial hamstring
and force placed down on L platform.
B) R glutes (abduction) with L glute during extension and engagement of R LT/Triceps and L
abs


What are we inhibiting?
    A) Back extensors, especially R QL/paravertebrals
    B) L pec minor

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    A) Low back tension/pain, weak quads, overused L VL secondary to disuse of L glute
       med/min and limited strength.
    B) Repositioning of L AIC pattern, weak L FA IR’s in hip extension.

Other notes:
The block under one foot enhances hemi- vestibular-postural demands.
All ‘dynamic’ activity requires single leg adaptation to force secondary to mass acceleration on
the block. The adaptation of the body and the brain, to the force placed on that block, enhances
the processing of mass distribution, so your brain now is geared into that site. (See the following
book for more information - Petroski H. Force: What it means to push and pull, slip and grip,
start and stop.)
Pole in R hand increases single leg compression on L foot when on L foot, and abdominal
compression from L abs when on R foot.
Pole is primarily used for balance purposes.

                                  Standing Supported Bilateral IO/TA


Reference: L > R Heel (Low)


Referent(s): L > R Ischial Tuberosity (Mod), IOs/TAs (High)


Cross-Referencing: Sense of quads with serratus anterior with abs; Sense of quads with abs
(exhaling) with dorsiflexors


What are we inhibiting?
    1) Glossopharyngeal muscle
    2) Tongue
    3) Shoulder elevators
    4) Upper traps
    5) Lats
    6) Plantarflexors

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Forward Head
    2) Sleep apnea
    3) Laryngeal tension – activities that pull larynx/hyoid down with posterior cranial and
       pelvis rotation
    4) Swallowing issues

Other notes:
Good technique to redirect sense of retro base of support.
Functional Squat Test of 3 and above allows abs to rotate torso (trunk).
Arms go above head with eccentric hamstrings and adductors.
Great position to introduce R arm reach  L arm reach, etc.

                       Standing Left Knee Flexion with Right AIC Progression


Reference: L Heel (Low) with L IO/TAs (High)  L glute med/min (pic 4)


Referent(s): Right Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Triceps) (Mod) with Left Anterior AF
Capsule (L Adductor) (High) (pic 4)


Cross-Referencing: L quad with R psoas hip flexor with L abs; L post mediastinum with L ant.
AF capsule with R lateral posterior upper chest with L ischial tuberosity


What are we inhibiting?
    1) Latero-spatial sense for single leg transfer of weight
    2) Hip Flexors
    3) Right Quads

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) SLR above 90° bilaterally (this technique inhibits hip flexors (psoas – especially L as R
       trunk rotators) and facilitates abs as rotators with hamstrings stabilizing pelvis for
       isolated torso rotation from abs
    2) Low back patients, SI patients (L or R)
    3) Patients with limited L shoulder flexion because this technique pulls L thorax under the L
       scapula
    4) R tennis elbox/lateral epicondylitis secondary to over-supination
    5) R quadricep overuse – grounding with L quads, not right quads in this technique

Other notes:
Stay mindful of ground (sight, sense of lift) coming up on the left only secondary to R UE
position of extension.
“Long seated” upright position for tentative “back” patients.

                                  Standing Supported Left Glute Push


Reference: L Ischial Tuberosity (Mod)/glute med/min


Referent(s): Right Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Triceps) (Mod), L Posterior
Mediastinum (High), L Ant AF Capsule (L Adductor) (High), L IOs/TAs (High)


Cross-Referencing: R hip flexor with R lower trap/triceps with L ab wall; L IO/TAs with sense
of R low trap with sense of L ischial seat (hamstrings) and L glute med/min


What are we inhibiting?
    1) Anterior neck, scalenes, accessory respiratory muscle

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Autonomic dysfunction (dysautonomia)

Other notes:
This technique is designed to place one in a ‘Valsalve-like’ maneuver position between the
exhalation and the inhalation phases, without blowing up a balloon and holding the expelled air
or while pinching off the nose.
By lifting the R LE, one activates both R psoas and R FA ER (glute max) to keep body mass
over to the left.
*See two-page handout from the PRI Breathing Mechanics in COVID Times webinar (week 3)

                               Modified Two Point Gait with Left AF IR


Reference: R Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Triceps) (Mod) and L Ischial Tuberosity
(Mod)


Referent(s): L IO/TAs and R Post Hip (R Glute Max)


Cross-Referencing: R toes with L adductor (pic 1); L heel with R triceps (pic 2); L ischial
tuberosity with R post hip/glute max (pic 3); R med long arch with L abs (pic 4)


What are we inhibiting?
    1) R UE with R LE and L UE with L LE ambulatory pattern

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Ataxia
    2) Scissor gait

Other notes:
The walking sticks are meant to slow pelvis timing with scapular alternation.
The left pole, left foot (i.e. L ischial seat and L calcaneus), and left abdominals all are modified
by the pole/stick in the right hand that, as a lever with the R triceps allows the R low trap and
pelvic floor to engage, control cognitive function and visuo-spatial re-orientation associated with
Left AF IR.
Thus, the L UE is no longer a cross reference with the R LE, and rather the L UE is assisting
finding left-sided awareness and L AF IR.

      Standing Right Knee Flexion with Resisted Left Hamstring and Right Trunk Rotation


Reference: R Med Long Arch (Low)


Referent(s): L Ischial Tuberosity (Mod), R Lateral Posterior Upper Chest (R LT/Triceps) (Mod)


Cross-Referencing: L hamstring with R quad and R glute max


What are we inhibiting?
    1) L hip flexors
    2) R SCM
    3) R BC muscles (R pecs)
    4) R hip adductors
    5) L trunk/torso rotation or R IOs/TAs

Who has Ron used this technique for and why?
    1) Right patellofemoral pain/quad tendonitis
    2) Patients who need R LE centering during L hip adduction
    3) Bilateral lats user (back over extension) - individuals who need integration of R thoracic/
       R scapula elevation through elevation of the L LE with L abs and L hip adductors

Other notes:
Mimics resisted decline retro walking using:
      - L abs
      - R floor is moving back and R foot is moving forward during L hip extension
