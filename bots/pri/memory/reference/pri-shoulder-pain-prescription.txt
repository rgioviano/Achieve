================================================================================
MOST COMMON OVERHEAD SPORT SHOULDER PROBLEMS
================================================================================
- Bicipital Tendonitis
- Shoulder Impingement

MOST COMMON REASON FOR OVERHEAD SHOULDER ISSUES
- The center of mass does not shift adequately over the "stance" leg or contralateral leg, as the thorax abducts under the side that the shoulder is moving into flexion.

================================================================================
MECHANICAL ISSUES - RIGHT BICIPITAL TENDONITIS
================================================================================
1. Decreased ability to rotate trunk or chest wall to the right.
2. Decreased ability to externally rotate ribs (turn ribs up in the front and retract rib cage, usually on the right secondary to forward shoulder position and weak serratus anterior) during integrated left abdominal (IO/TA) co-contraction.
   - See asterisked (*) activities on the techniques page.
3. Overuse of the latissimus dorsi for humeral (arm) internal rotation secondary to poor scapular position and therefore subscapularis and serratus muscle function.

Key muscles illustrated (Travell & Simons): Serratus Anterior, Subscapularis, Latissimus Dorsi.

================================================================================
MECHANICAL ISSUES - RIGHT SHOULDER IMPINGEMENT
================================================================================
1. Poor ability to expand rib cage with diaphragmatic breathing.
2. Poor ability to co-contract abdominals upon inhalation.
3. Over-activity from the latissimus for indirect control of scapula through thoracic extension, accompanying scapular posterior tilting and scapular downward rotation.
4. Weak low trap and long head of the triceps (usually on the right) resulting in winging and anterior tilting of the shoulder blade.

Also contribute to intra-scapular weakness (muscles between the shoulder blades).
Key muscles illustrated: Trapezius, Triceps (Travell & Simons).

TECHNIQUES FOR RIGHT BICIPITAL TENDONITIS WITH LEFT IO/TA's
================================================================================
(* = especially for rib ER / serratus / R trunk themes called out on mechanics page)

   (Integration – Standing #39)
2. *Standing Wall Supported Resisted Serratus Punch
   (Integration – Standing #33)
3. Latissimus Hang with Low Trap
   (Right Lower Trapezius & Right Tricep – Standing #24)
4. *Left Sidelying Weighted Right HG Horizontal Abduction with Right FA ER
   (Right Lower Trapezius & Right Tricep – Sidelying #4)
5. Standing Resisted Bilateral Serratus Press Through
   (Integration – Standing #61)
6. *Left Sidelying Resisted Right HG IR
   (Integration – Sidelying #63)
7. Supine Resisted Right HG IR with Left HG ER
   (Right Lower Trapezius & Right Tricep – Supine #16)
8. Seated Resisted Right Abducted Diagonal Extension
   (Right Lower Trapezius & Right Tricep – Seated #4)
9. *Left Sidelying Resisted Right Serratus Punch with Right Trunk Rotation
   (Right Lower Trapezius & Right Tricep – Sidelying #7)
10. *Standing Resisted Right Diagonal Flexion in Right AIC
    (Integration – Standing #73)
11. Reversed "C" Push-Ups
    (Integration – Supine #35)
