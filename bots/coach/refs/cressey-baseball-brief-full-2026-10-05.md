## 1. Question that would change the answer

**Can Achieve reliably assess each athlete's shoulder profile and track his throwing workload? Profile means total-motion IR+ER, laxity (Beighton), and scapular resting position. Workload means innings/pitches, two-way or catcher/SS duty, and months fully off throwing. Almost every Cressey prescription below branches on those two inputs. Examples: who gets stretched vs. stabilized, how much arm care, and whether weighted balls are even allowed.**

## 2. High-value implementables

### A. In-season scheduling (pitchers vs. position players)

**A1. 7-day starter template (HS and college once-a-week starter)**
- **Positions / phase:** Pitchers, in-season.
- **What to do (Paraphrase):**
  - Day 0: pitch.
  - Day 1: challenging lower-body lift plus light cuff work.
  - Day 2: movement training (10–15 yd starts, agility, some 50–60 yd top-speed work) plus an upper-body lift.
  - Day 3: low-intensity resistance circuits (<30% 1RM) and extended dynamic flexibility.
  - Day 4: full-body lift.
  - Day 5: movement training.
  - Day 6: low-intensity dynamic flexibility only, or off.
  - Day 7: pitch.
  - Dynamic flexibility and static stretching happen every day.
  - Most weeks have only 2 sprint days, never more than 3.
- **HS variant (Part 2, 2011):**
  - Mon: pitch.
  - Tue: lower-emphasis lift plus core and light upper.
  - Wed: movement.
  - Thu: low-volume med ball plus an upper-emphasis lift (low-volume lower).
  - Fri: movement.
  - Sat: very light lift (mostly upper and core).
  - Sun: off.
  - If he plays the field on non-pitching days, drop the movement sessions and cut either the Thu or the Sat lift.
- **Sources:**
  - *A New Model for Training Between Starts: Part 2*, EC, site date 2009-01-01. https://ericcressey.com/a-new-model-for-training-between-starts-part-2/
  - *In-Season Baseball Strength and Conditioning: Part 2 – High School Baseball*, EC, 2011-03-22. https://ericcressey.com/in-season-baseball-strength-and-conditioning-part-2-high-school-baseball/
- **Extra day or pushed start:**
  - On a 7-day rotation, lift on Days 1, 3, and 5, with Day 5 lighter.
  - If an extra day appears, split the Day 5 lift into upper and lower sessions, or keep it as is and add med ball circuits on Day 6.
  - If the start gets pushed *up*, drop the Day 5 lift.
  - Source: *What to Do With an Extra Day Between Pitching Starts*, EC, 2012-04-24. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-strength-and-conditioning-extra-day-between-pitching-starts/

**A2. 5-day template (pro starters; useful for summer leagues and short rotations)**
- **What to do (Paraphrase):**
  - Day 0: pitch.
  - Day 1: challenging lower-body lift, light push-up variation, light horizontal pulling, cuff work.
  - Day 2: movement training only.
  - Day 3: bullpen, single-leg work, challenging upper-body lift (less vertical pulling in-season), cuff work.
  - Day 4: low-intensity dynamic flexibility circuits only.
  - Day 5: pitch.
  - If he is recovering poorly, drop Day 2 and do the sprints after the Day 3 bullpen.
- **Source:** *A New Model for Training Between Starts: Part 2* (link above). The pro version is restated in *In-Season … Part 4 – Professional Baseball*, EC, 2011-03-24. https://ericcressey.com/in-season-baseball-strength-and-conditioning-part-4-professional-baseball/

**A3. Relievers: give the chaos a fixed structure**
- **Positions:** Relief pitchers. College and pro.
- **College (Paraphrase):**
  - Every reliever lifts Monday and Thursday, even if he may pitch Thursday night. Just pare back the volume.
  - Don't add new exercises, so he doesn't get sore.
  - After a long outing, treat him like a starter and lift within 12 hours.
  - Source: *In-Season … Part 3 – College Baseball*, EC, 2011-03-23. https://ericcressey.com/in-season-baseball-strength-and-conditioning-part-3-college-baseball/
- **Pro option menu (Paraphrase):**
  - Long full-body option: 15–17 sets.
  - Two short full-body options (lower emphasis or upper emphasis): 8–12 sets each.
  - One movement day.
  - Over 20 pitches in an outing: do Day 1 and then Day 2 over the next two days.
  - Under 20 pitches: go straight to Short Option 1.
  - Source: Part 4 (link above).
- **Summer-league relievers:**
  - Train them like position players.
  - If they will likely throw within 24–36 hours, cut volume and intensity sharply and aim for "refreshed."
  - Source: *Training Schedules for Summer Baseball*, EC, 2010-05-31. https://ericcressey.com/training-schedules-for-summer-baseball-pitchers/

**A4. Position players and catchers in-season**
- **HS (Paraphrase):**
  - Usually two full-body lifts per week. For a MWF game schedule, a short session Tue and a longer one Sat.
  - No extra movement training; practice covers it.
  - Keep med ball volume low.
  - Foam rolling and mobility daily.
- **College (Paraphrase):**
  - Hardest lift on Monday after the weekend series.
  - Second lift Wed or Thu.
- **Catchers catching 4–5 games/week (Paraphrase):**
  - No squatting in-season; use deadlift variations and single-leg work.
  - Lift post-game when possible.
- **Sources:** Parts 2, 3, and 4 (links above).

**A5. In-season session size and selection rules**
- **Positions:** Both.
- **Quote:** "Rarely will an in-season strength training program session last more than 35-40 minutes. It's usually roughly 10-14 sets worth of work."
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Keep week 1 of a new in-season program lighter to limit soreness, then load more in weeks 2–4.
  - Use more rows than chin-ups or pull-ups. Vertical pulling at most once a week in-season, because some players get "cranky" elbows.
  - For pitchers, use more push-ups than DB bench. With 3 pushing days, make two of them push-ups.
  - Use only a little med ball work, mostly *opposite* the throwing/hitting direction, plus a little overhead work.
  - No distance running.
- **Source:** *In-Season Baseball Strength and Conditioning: Part 1*, EC, 2011-03-21. https://ericcressey.com/in-season-baseball-strength-and-conditioning-part-1/
- **CSP-staff guest supplement** (John O'Neil, *7 Ways to Maintain Strength During Baseball Season*, 2018-02-25, https://ericcressey.com/7-ways-to-maintain-strength-during-baseball-season/), Paraphrase:
  - Track body weight in-season.
  - Stack lifts on hard on-field days and keep a true off day.
  - 20–40 min micro-sessions are fine. Sample tri-set session: deadlift or squat 3×3 / arm care / core, then single-leg / push / pull.
  - Avoid brand-new exercises and heavy eccentrics in-season (e.g., step-ups over Bulgarian split squats).
  - Rotate the main lift's emphasis week to week: heavy, then strength-speed, then speed-strength.

### B. Arm care: what, how much, when

**B1. Cuff work = strength + timing, not endless band reps**
- **Positions:** Pitchers mainly. Position-player throwers too.
- **Quote:** "Most people don't appreciate that throwing a baseball IS rotator cuff training."
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Most higher-level throwers have cuff *timing* problems more than strength problems (a point from PT Eric Schoenberg that EC endorses).
  - Bands put the most resistance at full external rotation, where throwers are weakest, and people tend to rush the eccentric.
  - Prioritize two drill types:
    - **Rhythmic stabilizations.** Gentler perturbations close to the shoulder for lax athletes, firmer and farther down the arm for stable ones. Lax athletes can start closed-chain. Keep the scapula off the rib cage, but not cranked into retraction. Use 2×/week in the off-season, between sets on lower-body days.
    - **Manual-resistance external rotations.** Cue "pick it up early." Emphasize the eccentric, but don't push all the way down. Cup the elbow, and watch for wrist-extensor cheating. Use 2×/week in the off-season near the end of upper-body days. Cut back when throwing ramps up, because they cause soreness.
- **Sources:**
  - *Baseball S&C Programs: How Much Rotator Cuff Work is Too Much? Part 1*, EC, 2012-01-09. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-strength-and-conditioning-programs-rotator-cuff-work-1/
  - *… Part 2*, EC, 2012-01-11. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-strength-and-conditioning-programs-rotator-cuff-work-2/

**B2. Classify every cuff drill, then dose often and lightly** (latest view, 2018)
- **Five categories (Paraphrase):**
  - **Strength:** manual resistance, cables, dumbbells, loaded and challenging.
  - **Timing:** 90/90 holds, rhythmic stabilizations.
  - **Endurance:** EC calls this more important for swimmers than for baseball players.
  - **Irradiation:** e.g., bottoms-up kettlebell work.
  - **Patterning:** full-range humeral motion, especially end-range ER.
- **Frequency (Quote):** "I prefer near-daily exposures rather than exhaustive, less frequent programs."
  - For pro guys: some cuff work 5–6 days/week. Strength and irradiation 2×/week, timing 2×/week, patterning nearly daily.
  - He criticizes the common 2×/week, 45–60 min, ~15-exercise routines.
- **Coaching point (Paraphrase):** Don't let the arm drift back into horizontal abduction during ER work. That turns on the posterior deltoid, which glides the humeral head *forward*. Athletes who feel ER drills in the front of the shoulder are reinforcing a bad pattern.
- **Source:** *3 Random Thoughts on Rotator Cuff Readiness*, EC, 2018-10-21. https://ericcressey.com/3-rotator-cuff-readiness/

**B3. Train the positions that actually matter**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Loaded carries and Turkish get-ups are useful but *not enough* for throwers.
  - Add **90/90 external rotation holds** (late-cocking position) and **rhythmic stabilization at the ball-release position** (where distraction forces act).
  - Build strength first, then velocity-end challenges.
  - EC cites isometric research showing strength carryover of about 10–15° from the trained joint angle.
- **Source:** *Common Arm Care Mistakes – Installment 6*, EC, 2015-04-23. https://ericcressey.com/common-arm-care-mistakes-installment-6/

**B4. Pre-throw warm-up: 15–24 minutes, sorted by athlete type**
- **Positions:** Pitchers and position players.
- **Paraphrase, by type:**
  - **Tight** guys get the longest warm-up (≤20 min): length first, then activation (anterior core, posterior cuff, scapular upward rotators).
  - **Loose / hypermobile** guys get ≤15 min: no stretching and little ground-based mobility. Get them standing and moving, with prone ER and rhythmic stabilizations.
  - **Middle-of-the-road** guys get 15–20 min.
- **Generic template (Paraphrase):**
  - Foam roll: 5 min.
  - Mobility drills: 8–10 min.
  - Scapular control (wall-slide variations, prone trap raises): 2 min.
  - Cuff activation (prone ER, rhythmic stabs, maybe 1–2 band sets): 2 min.
  - Easy movement and sprint build-ups: 5 min.
- **Quote (EC citing former CSP pitching coordinator Matt Blake):** "If you need a post-workout shake after your warm-up, you're doing it all wrong."
- **Source:** *Common Arm Care Mistakes: Installment 2*, EC, 2013-12-19. https://ericcressey.com/common-arm-care-mistakes-installment-2/
- **Also (Quote):** "You warm up to throw; you don't throw to warm up." He has athletes do 8–10 dynamic flexibility drills plus easy sprint progressions before picking up a ball.
  - Source: *Understanding Elbow Pain – Part 4: Protecting Pitchers*, EC, 2010-05-21. https://ericcressey.com/understanding-elbow-pain-part-4-protecting-pitchers/

**B5. Arm care volume moves opposite to throwing volume**
- **Quote:** "If you keep adding things without taking something else away, you'll eventually wind up with overuse problems."
- **Paraphrase:**
  - As pitch counts and throwing rise, cut upper-body lifting volume, med ball volume, *and* arm care volume.
  - Example: MLB closer Steve Cishek's off-season med ball totals (left + right + overhead throws):
    - October: no aggressive med ball or overhead work.
    - November: 156 throws.
    - December (throwing started mid-month): 138 throws.
    - January: 66 throws.
- **Source:** *Common Arm Care Mistakes: Installment 4*, EC, 2014-01-30. https://ericcressey.com/common-arm-care-mistakes-installment-4/

**B6. Post-outing priority: restore range of motion**
- **Positions:** Pitchers.
- **Paraphrase:**
  - The #1 priority in the 24 h after an outing is regaining shoulder IR and elbow extension, and loosening the hips. No "flush runs."
  - EC cites Reinold et al.: pitchers lose an average 9.5° of shoulder IR and 3.2° of elbow extension right after pitching, and the losses persist at 24 h.
  - His preferred self-stretch: the **side-lying cross-body stretch** (scapula stabilized, gentle, consistent). He uses it more than the sleeper because form is harder to butcher.
  - Light band "blood-flow" work: he is "pretty apathetic" about it.
  - Caveat: some athletes should *not* be stretched into IR (see Don'ts).
- **Sources:**
  - *Band Work after Pitching?*, EC, 2009-12-04. https://ericcressey.com/band-work-after-pitching/
  - *7 Reasons Baseball Pitchers Shouldn't Do Year-Round Throwing Programs – Part 2*, EC, 2011-12-01. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-pitchers-year-round-throwing-programs-2/
  - Elbow Part 4 (link above).

**B7. Two-way and youth usage rules**
- **Positions:** Both. HS and youth.
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Starters throwing 60+ pitches at least once a week should play DH/1B/3B/2B/OF in the 2–3 days after a start, and preferably all week. Not SS or C.
  - Relievers throwing 15–20 pitches a few times a week can play anywhere.
  - Regular HS pitchers should avoid catching and SS/3B.
  - A kid pitching fewer than 3 innings/week trains like a position player, with one lift the day after he throws.
- **Sources:**
  - *Common Arm Care Mistakes: Installment 5*, EC, 2014-03-06. https://ericcressey.com/common-arm-care-mistakes-installment-5/
  - In-Season Part 2 (link above).

### C. Shoulder mobility, scapula, T-spine

**C1. Assess before you stretch the throwing shoulder**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Most pitchers already have extra dominant-side ER, and therefore anterior instability.
  - Only add ER mobility after measuring total motion (IR+ER) and finding an *asymmetry* that calls for it (e.g., dominant-side ER is lower). Even then, pair it with scapular stabilization and T-spine extension/rotation, as in the side-lying extension-rotation drill.
  - Hypermobile athletes (high Beighton score) need stability work, not sleeper stretches.
- **Source:** *Mobilizing the Throwing Shoulder: The Do and Don't*, EC, 2010-06-18. https://ericcressey.com/mobilizing-the-throwing-shoulder-the-do-and-dont/

**C2. Thoracic mobility for throwers, progressed through the off-season**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Pick drills that move the T-spine *independent of the arm*, especially early off-season, when the anterior shoulder is "stretched out."
  - **Off-season months 1–2 and in-season:** supine alternating shoulder flexion on a doubled tennis ball, thoracic extension on a roller, rock-back quadruped extension-rotation.
  - **Months 3–4:** side-lying windmill, bent-over T-spine rotation (more humeral movement).
  - Order: after foam rolling and positional breathing, before scap and cuff work.
  - Why it matters: lacking T-spine rotation makes the arm drag and pushes ER into the wrong places. Lacking T-spine extension leads to lumbar extension, linked to low back pain, oblique strains, and hip issues.
- **Source:** *Improving Thoracic Mobility in Throwers*, EC, 2013-11-05. https://ericcressey.com/thoracic-spine-mobility-drills-throwers/

**C3. Scapula: cue upward rotation, not "down and back," once the arm moves**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Resting scapular posture differs between athletes. Some are anteriorly tilted, abducted, and depressed. Others sit too adducted (medial border should be about 3 in from the spine).
  - "Down and back" can bias the lats, or fight upward rotation.
  - Throwers need a scapula that is stable but free to move on the rib cage. Don't pin it down with the lats during pulling.
- **Sources (EC):**
  - *Common Arm Care Mistakes: Installment 1*, 2013-12-13. https://ericcressey.com/common-arm-care-mistakes-installment-1/
  - *Installment 3*, 2014-01-17 (video post). https://ericcressey.com/common-arm-care-mistakes-installment-3/
- **CSP-staff guest** (PT Eric Schoenberg, *Arm Care: Why Are We Still Talking About "Down and Back?"*, 2020-03-09, https://ericcressey.com/arm-care-down-and-back/), Paraphrase:
  - Use "down and back" only when the arms are at the sides (deadlifts, carries).
  - Once the humerus moves more than 20–30° from the side, cue some upward rotation and elevation.
  - Release-point extension can differ by 3–4 inches depending on scapular position.
- **Drill choice:** forearm wall-slide variations are "among our favorites" for dynamic scapular stability.
  - Source: *Early Off-Season Priorities 6-10*, EC, 2011-09-21. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-strength-and-conditioning-off-season-6-10/

### D. Throwing programs (only what Cressey/CSP say)

**D1. Long toss: yes, structured correctly**
- **Positions:** Pitchers (concepts also fit position players).
- **Paraphrase:**
  - EC is "a big fan." His case: it raises arm speed, trains in a "generally-specific" way, improves lower-half contribution, gives a measurable marker (distance), keeps throwing fun, and is slightly *less* specific than mound work, which he says helps health.
  - **Structure it right:**
    - Finish with **compression/pull-down throws on the way back in**, about every 45–60 ft. Example: out to 300 ft, then compression throws at about 250/200/150/100/60 ft.
    - Throw **on a straight line** (foul line, or between the foul poles).
    - Control-challenged pitchers can follow with a short flat-ground or regular pen.
  - Cut back long toss if the athlete throws far but pitches slow, walks people, or lacks secondary pitches.
- **Sources:**
  - *Making the Case for Long Toss in a Throwing Program*, EC, 2010-12-12. https://ericcressey.com/making-the-case-for-long-toss-in-a-throwing-program/
  - *Throwing Programs: The Top 4 Long Toss Mistakes*, EC, 2010-12-14. https://ericcressey.com/the-top-4-long-toss-mistakes/

**D2. Weighted balls: tightly constrained**
- **Positions:** Advanced pitchers only: pro, college, and "more advanced high school guys."
- **Paraphrase / quote fragments:**
  - Only for athletes with a strength base who have mastered the regular 5 oz ball. "NOT something … to just implement on a gross scale with unprepared 13-year-old kids."
  - Throw into a net from about 6–8 ft. Never off a mound, never during long toss, and never play catch with them.
  - Start only after long toss is built up, around when bullpens begin.
  - Do them after long toss, after a bullpen, or as a stand-alone session. Never before a bullpen.
  - Use 7–11 oz, and end each set with the 5 oz ball (e.g., 3 at 7 oz, 3 at 9 oz, 3 at 5 oz).
  - No more than 8 oz on drills with significant lay-back.
  - Example season window: a pro prospect's weighted-ball work ran Jan 11 to Feb 18.
- **Source:** *Weighted Baseballs: Safe and Effective, or Stupid and Dangerous?*, EC, 2009-12-15. https://ericcressey.com/weighted-baseballs-safe-and-effective-or-stupid-and-dangerous/
- **Later caution (EC, 2018):** weighted-ball programs raise ER quickly, especially in hypermobile throwers, and EC lists them among the reasons for the rise in lat strains. Quote: "Weighted balls are awesome – when they're integrated at the right times, at the right loads, in the right dosages, with the right athletes who have earned the right to use them."
  - Source: *5 Reasons for the Increase in Lat Strains in Baseball Pitchers*, EC, 2018-07-09. https://ericcressey.com/5-reasons-lat-strains-baseball-pitchers/
- **CSP-staff guest** (James Cerbie, *Aggressive Throwing Programs: Are You Asking the Right Questions?*, 2014-10-05, https://ericcressey.com/aggressive-throwing-programs/): build the "pyramid base" first (general strength and movement) before an aggressive weighted-ball program. Includes a screening checklist (see Section 3).

**D3. Annual throwing calendar and time off**
- **Quote:** "pitchers need the ball completely out of their hands for at least two month per year, preferably continuously." (sic)
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Eight separate one-week breaks are not equivalent.
  - His reasons for the break:
    - Lose some ER to regain anterior stability.
    - Rebuild cuff and scapular strength.
    - Do manual-resistance cuff work, which is too sore-making to combine with throwing.
    - Restore shoulder and elbow ROM.
    - Let accumulated eccentric damage and low-grade injuries settle.
- **Sources (EC):**
  - *7 Reasons … Year-Round Throwing – Part 1*, 2011-11-29. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-pitchers-year-round-throwing-programs-1/
  - *Part 2*, 2011-12-01 (link above).
- **Individualizing the throwing start (Paraphrase):**
  - The more innings last year, the later he starts.
  - Relievers start earlier than starters.
  - Med ball volume goes down as throwing goes up.
  - No single throwing program goes to everyone.
  - Source: *Throwing Programs: Not One-Size-Fits-All*, EC, 2010-11-09. https://ericcressey.com/throwing-programs-not-one-size-fits-all/
- **Youth / HS calendar (Paraphrase):**
  - Ages 9–14: baseball 4–5 months a year, with at least two other sports the rest of the year ("rule of thirds"). Strength training year-round is fine.
  - Ages 15–16: two sports. Baseball about 7–8 months including preparation. No fall ball.
  - Ages 17+: specializing is OK, but throw only 8–9 months a year at most. For specialized HS players, roughly August through October off.
  - EC cites a study in which throwing more than 100 innings a year at ages 9–14 was associated with 3.5× the risk of elbow or shoulder surgery.
  - No off-season showcases. If attending one, pick June to early August.
  - Source: *Your Arm Hurts? Thank Your Little League, AAU, and Fall Ball Coaches*, EC, 2011-04-26. https://ericcressey.com/your-arm-hurts-thank-your-coaches/
- **CSP-staff guest** (Matt Blake, *Long-Term Baseball Development: Part 2*, 2014-05-10, https://ericcressey.com/long-term-baseball-development-part-2/), Paraphrase:
  - 8–12 weeks of no throwing each year.
  - HS pitchers under 100 competitive innings, "hopefully more like 80."
  - College pitchers: choose between summer ball and fall development rather than stacking 80+ spring innings, 50+ summer innings, and a fall season.

### E. Rotational power / medicine ball

**E1. Five-step rotational med ball progression (earn each step)**
- **Steps (Paraphrase):**
  1. Stationary anti-rotation, e.g., split-stance anti-rotation scoop toss. Teaches bracing on the front leg and T-spine rotation.
  2. Stationary rotation, e.g., rotational shotput.
  3. Momentum rotation, e.g., step-behind rotational shotput. Cue: "riding a bike into a curb."
  4. Eccentric pre-loading, e.g., step-back rotational scoop toss.
  5. Pre-loading plus momentum, e.g., 2-hop to rotational scoop toss.
- **Quote:** "Steps 3-5 are far to advanced for 13-15-year-old athletes." (sic)
- Even pros start at the early steps in their first off-season with CSP.
- **Positions:** Both.
- **Source:** *Making Sense of Rotational Medicine Ball Progressions*, EC, 2018-11-17. https://ericcressey.com/making-sense-of-rotational-medicine-ball-progressions/

**E2. Coaching cues (Quote, EC)**
- "Keep the head behind the belly button a bit longer." Preserves hip-shoulder separation. Leaking forward makes a pitcher's arm "late."
- "Make your front leg and back legs work like a slingshot." The front foot must stiffen to accept force, so watch for athletes rolling over the side of the shoe.
- "Take your hand to the wall." Prevents wrist redirection and getting "around" the ball on scoop tosses.
- **Source:** *Coaching Cues … Installment 13*, EC, 2015-12-20. https://ericcressey.com/3-coaching-cues-strength-and-conditioning-programs-13/

**E3. Load, volume, frequency, timing**
- **Load:**
  - Quote: "throwing medicine balls isn't lifting weights … For most folks, in the context of rotational power work, the appropriate load is 4-8lb."
  - If technique breaks down, lighten the ball first.
  - Overhead stomps can go heavier, especially bilateral.
  - Med ball work is mostly concentric, so it rarely causes soreness and can be trained often.
  - "Layering" progression across sets: scoop toss, then shuffle to scoop toss, then change of direction to shuffle to scoop toss, then double-play scoop toss.
  - Source: *Random Thoughts on Sports Performance Training – Installment 37*, EC, 2021-06-18. https://ericcressey.com/random-thoughts-sports-performance-training-37/
- **Late off-season:** as throwing and hitting ramp up, use "combination" overhead + rotational drills with a 6–10 lb ball (Jan–Feb for pros).
  - Source: *Programming Principles: Installment 6*, EC, 2021-12-17. https://ericcressey.com/programming-principles-installment-6/
- **Volume:**
  - Up to 240–360 med ball throws/week in the highest-volume phases (Oct–Jan for pros).
  - EC prefers med ball and non-sagittal jumps over Olympic lifts, because "power development is extremely plane-specific."
  - Source: *5 Things that Might Surprise You about our Baseball Strength and Conditioning Programs*, EC, 2012-05-23. https://ericcressey.com/5-things-that-might-surprise-you-about-our-baseball-strength-and-conditioning-programs/
- **CSP-staff guest** (Jordan Kraus, *Progression Strategies for Back Hip Loading*, 2020-05-19, https://ericcressey.com/progression-strategies-for-back-hip-loading/): back-hip load = flexion, adduction, and internal rotation, progressed sagittal → frontal → transverse.
  - **Unloaded:** RDL/1-leg RDL → lateral lunge → bowler squat.
  - **Loaded:** KB RDL/1-leg KB RDL → KB lateral lunge → rotational landmine press/rotational row.
  - **Dynamic:** drop squat 2:1 → low box shuffle w/stick → lateral lunge w/fake med ball chop.
  - **Skill-specific:** RFE med ball shotput → step-back shotput → knee-to-knee shotput.

### F. Strength, lower body, conditioning

**F1. Pitchers need strength training**
- **Quote:** "You've got to train ass to throw gas!"
- **Paraphrase:**
  - EC cites the DeRenne et al. 2001 review: in 22 of 26 summarized studies, throwing plus resistance training increased velocity more than throwing alone.
  - **Preferred options:** push-up variations, neutral-grip (multi-purpose bar) and DB benching, all rows and chin-ups, thick-handle grip work, med ball throws, safety-squat / giant cambered bar squats, front squats, deadlift variations.
- **Source:** *Strength Training for Pitchers*, EC, 2009-01-02. https://ericcressey.com/strength-training-for-pitchers-2/

**F2. Early off-season priorities (applies after a HS or college season too)**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Plan the off-season around last season's innings.
  - Master the sagittal plane first: anti-extension and anti-rotation core, single-leg work. Some athletes skip med ball for the first month.
  - Rebuild the cuff during the no-throw window.
  - Restore mobility. Tools: soft-tissue work, breathing/PRI-style respiration work, low-load long-duration static stretching where tissues are truly short, then strength work to "hold" the new range.
  - Dynamic scapular stability.
  - Global strength (deadlift variations, single-leg, squats "in some of our guys," pull-ups, rows, push-ups, DB bench), with reduced reactive work at first.
- **Sources (EC):**
  - *Early Off-Season Priorities 1-5*, 2011-09-20. https://ericcressey.com/baseball-strength-and-conditioning-off-season-1-5/
  - *6-10*, 2011-09-21 (link above).
- **Note:** EC later changed part of this. See the dated disagreement in Section 5.

**F3. Power detrains fastest, and strength comes first for youth**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Keep sprint, jump, and change-of-direction volume intact during deload weeks.
  - Restart tempo runs, pogo jumps, foundational deceleration progressions, and med ball work right away in the early off-season.
  - Quote: "It's always easier to make a fast guy strong than it is to make a strong guy fast."
  - Source: *Programming Principles: Installment 6* (link above).
- **Youth:**
  - Maximal strength is the base of EC's "pyramid" of athletic qualities.
  - He endorses at least 2 strength days/week from about age 11 (citing Mike Boyle).
  - Source: *Strength in the Teenage Years: An Overlooked Long-Term Athletic Development Competitive Advantage*, EC, 2018-07-31. https://ericcressey.com/strength-in-the-teenage-years-an-overlooked-long-term-athletic-development-competitive-advantage/
- **Age bands (Paraphrase):**
  - Ages 9–12: largely the same program for everyone.
  - Ages 13–15: about 2×/week strength and conditioning.
  - Ages 16–18: frequency ramps up.
  - College: athletes often gain fast in years 1–2, then regress in years 3–4 if heavy bilateral loading isn't individualized.
  - Source: *Individualization and the College Baseball Athlete*, EC, 2024-05-09. https://ericcressey.com/individualization-and-the-college-baseball-athlete/

**F4. Conditioning: short and fast, or slow and mobile, never the middle**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - Keep longer-duration work below about 70% HRR (ideally drills with big joint ranges).
  - Do everything else at 90%+ effort: starts, agilities, sprints up to 60 yd.
  - Build pitch-specific stamina by pitching and progressing pitch counts, supplemented by med ball medleys.
  - Quote: "I'd call anything over 150m 'distance running' in a pitching population."
- **Sources (EC):**
  - *A New Model for Training Between Starts: Part 1*, 2008-02-01. https://ericcressey.com/a-new-model-for-training-between-starts-part-1/
  - Part 2 (link above).
- **CSP-staff guest** (Rob Rabena, *Should Pitchers Distance Run? What the Research Says*, 2012-08-22, https://ericcressey.com/should-pitchers-distance-run-what-the-research-says/): suggests ≤40-yd sprints, 4–8 reps, 2–3×/week, plus lateral work such as band-resisted Heidens and lateral skips. See Section 5 for the evidence caveat.

**F5. Frontal-plane power and fielding**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - EC doesn't Olympic-lift his baseball players and does little vertical jumping. He uses rotational med ball work and lateral jumping/landing instead.
  - Guest interview (Graeme Lehman's master's research, 42 college players): the **lateral-to-medial jump** off the same-side leg as the throwing arm was the only test that correlated with throwing velocity in all 4 subgroups. Suggested additions: band-resisted lateral jumps, lateral sled drags, and single-leg work (concentric back leg, eccentric lead leg).
  - Source: *Developing Baseball Power: What the Latest Research Says*, EC with guest Q&A, 2012-08-14. https://ericcressey.com/developing-baseball-power-what-the-latest-research-says/
- **Fielders (non-CSP guest, Jason Feairheller, 2020-07-14):** multidirectional plyos for the hip turn, e.g., lateral bound with push-back at 3 angles, then hip turn at 3 angles, with emphasis on short ground contact.
  - Source: https://ericcressey.com/developing-multidirectional-power-for-the-baseball-fielder/

**F6. Core training for rotational athletes**
- **Paraphrase:**
  - For oblique-strain prevention, emphasize anti-rotation and anti-extension. Avoid sit-ups, crunches, and side bends.
  - The most common oblique injury site is the 11th-rib attachment on the non-throwing side. A flared rib cage from poor anterior core increases the stretch there.
- **Source:** *Oblique Strains in Baseball: 2011 Update*, EC, 2011-03-29. https://ericcressey.com/oblique-strains-in-baseball-2011-update/
- **Hitters (Paraphrase):**
  - The core *transfers* force rather than creating it, so favor rotational med ball work and chops/lifts.
  - Hitters need hip IR and extension, T-spine mobility for separation, and a hip hinge.
  - EC says he doubts many MLB hitters can't deadlift at least 1.5× body weight (opinion).
  - Source: *6 Physical Attributes of Elite Hitters*, EC, 2015-09-13. https://ericcressey.com/6-physical-attributes-elite-hitters/

## 3. Assessment / screening ideas Strength Coach can use

| Screen | What Cressey says to look for | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shoulder total motion (IR+ER), both sides** | Compare total motion side to side. His example of a symmetrical thrower: dominant 45° IR + 125° ER = 170°; non-dominant 55° IR + 115° ER = 170°. A 10° IR deficit is "completely normal" in throwers (retroversion). Look at total motion first, then decide whether IR, ER, or both need change. Quote: "It is better to be too tight than too loose!" | *Shoulder Range-of-Motion Norms*, EC, 2009-07-23. https://ericcressey.com/shoulder-range-of-motion-norms/ ; *Recap: Testing, Treating, and Training the Shoulder*, EC, 2009-11-18. https://ericcressey.com/recap-testing-treating-and-training-the-shoulder/ |
| **Need for ER work?** | Only if total motion is asymmetrical *and* dominant-side ER is lower. | *Mobilizing the Throwing Shoulder* (2010, link above) |
| **Laxity (Beighton)** | A high score means stability work and no sleeper stretch. Hypermobility becomes more common at higher levels. | Same |
| **Tight / loose / middle classification** | Sets warm-up length and content. | *Common Arm Care Mistakes 2* (2013) |
| **Resting scapular position** | Medial border about 3 in from the spine. Neutral is about 5° upward rotation. Downward rotation at rest is like starting "20 yards behind the starting line." | *Common Arm Care Mistakes 1* (2013); *Assessments You Might Be Overlooking: Installment 3*, EC, 2014-01-11. https://ericcressey.com/assessments-you-might-be-overlooking-installment-3/ |
| **Shoulder flexion** | Supine, back flat, knees bent: arms should reach the table. Also a back-to-wall shoulder flexion test. Limited flexion means no overhead pressing. Lots of athletes in the weight room with limited flexion is a red flag for lat-dominant programming. | Installment 3 (2014); *5 Reasons for the Increase in Lat Strains* (2018) |
| **Shrug pattern on overhead reach** | Refer to check for a rotator cuff tear. Never train through it. | *Recap: Testing, Treating…* (2009) |
| **Post-outing ROM** | Re-check shoulder IR and elbow extension, and restore them within 24 h. | *Year-Round Throwing Part 2* (2011); *Elbow Part 4* (2010) |
| **End-of-season deficit list** | Lead-leg hip extension, lead-leg hip IR, lead-leg knee flexion, throwing-shoulder IR, scapular posterior tilt, throwing-elbow extension. | *A New Model … Part 1* (2008) |
| **Seated hip IR** | Sit tall at the table edge, hips and knees at 90°, internally rotate the femur without letting the hip hike. | *Measuring Hip Internal Rotation*, EC, 2009-11-23. https://ericcressey.com/measuring-hip-internal-rotation/ |
| **Pain only on the mound after a clean rehab** | "CHECK THE HIPS!" Look for staying closed or flying open. | *Recap: Testing, Treating…* (2009) |
| **Thoracic rotation** | Lumbar-locked rotation (TPI). Use seated rotation as the regression if knee flexion is limited. Judge against sport and age demands, not population "norms." | *Stop Thinking About "Normal" Thoracic Spine Mobility*, EC, 2018-06-20. https://ericcressey.com/normal-thoracic-spine-mobility/ ; *10 More Important Notes on Assessments*, EC, 2017-12-09. https://ericcressey.com/10-more-important-notes-on-assessments/ |
| **Lateral flexion (QL)** | Limited side bend with the hip shifting out suggests the opposite-side QL. In a flat-T-spine athlete with limited rotation: if pec (horizontal abduction) and lat (shoulder flexion) check out, test QL. | *Assessments You Might Be Overlooking: Installment 6*, EC, 2018-11-02. https://ericcressey.com/assessments-you-might-be-overlooking-installment-6/ |
| **Toe touch / hip hinge (hitters)** | Should touch the toes without more than about 30° of knee bend or knee hyperextension. | *6 Physical Attributes of Elite Hitters* (2015) |
| **Watch them train** | Film arm care exercises and bullpens as part of the assessment. Watch for straining (jaw clenching, fist making). Health history first, conversation second, movement third. Refer out when something looks clinical. Weigh male athletes rather than taking their word. | *10 More Important Notes on Assessments* (2017) |
| **Rhythmic stabilization as a monitor** | Week-to-week changes in cuff control are easy to perceive. | *Rotator Cuff Work Part 2* (2012) |
| **Adolescent low back pain** | EC describes the standard sports-medicine rule: low back pain lasting more than 3 days in an adolescent athlete means referral for a bone scan to rule out spondylolysis. Higher risk in athletes who hyperextend. | *Spondylolysis and Young Athletes*, EC, site date 2008-01-17. https://ericcressey.com/newsletter114html/ |
| **Lateral-to-medial jump (candidate test)** | Correlated with throwing velocity in one 42-player college study (guest research). Treat as exploratory. | *Developing Baseball Power…* (2012) |
| **Pre-weighted-ball checklist (CSP-staff guest)** | Baseline movement, scapular upward rotation, overhead reach without lumbar extension, sagittal-plane strength, getting "in and out" of both hips, core control, single-leg stance, symmetrical total shoulder motion and flexion. | *Aggressive Throwing Programs* (2014) |

## 4. Clear don'ts / red flags (from his posts)

1. **No distance running for pitchers**, and no "flush runs." He counts anything over 150 m as distance in pitchers. Avoid the ~75%-HRR middle zone. (*New Model Part 1 & 2*, 2008/2009; *Elbow Part 4*, 2010)
2. **No broomstick dislocates.** They add valgus stress to the elbow. **Don't crank throwers into ER or horizontal abduction**, and don't use bands to pull arms into extreme positions. (*Mobilizing the Throwing Shoulder*, 2010; *5 Things…*, 2012)
3. **Don't prescribe sleeper stretches to everyone.** Lax athletes get irritated anterior capsules and biceps tendons. (2010)
4. **No kettlebell arm bar for throwers**, and avoid T-spine drills that drive humeral ER early in the off-season. (*Improving Thoracic Mobility in Throwers*, 2013)
5. **Don't fatigue the cuff before throwing.** No 30-exercise pre-throw circuits. Warm-up tops out around 20–24 min. (*Arm Care Mistakes 2*, 2013)
6. **No daily heavy tubing in-season** ("unless they are incredibly light"). Never train the cuff to failure (relayed from Mike Reinold). (*In-Season Part 1*, 2011; *Clearing up the Rotator Cuff Controversy*, EC, 2010-01-31, https://ericcressey.com/clearing-up-the-rotator-cuff-controversy/)
7. **Exercises he has listed as contraindicated or avoided for baseball:**
   - Straight-bar bench.
   - Upright rows.
   - Front and side raises, especially empty cans.
   - Olympic lifts (2009 allowed high pulls as the exception; by 2012 "we don't Olympic lift").
   - Barbell or DB overhead pressing: "I don't love these for baseball players" (2017).
   - Back squats on the 2009 list, front squats preferred.
   - See Section 5 for how this list evolved.
8. **Don't cue "down and back" for everything.** It locks the scapula; the lats take over. (2013–2014; Schoenberg guest 2020)
9. **Don't let lats creep into arm care**, such as tugging the humerus down on ER, prone T's, or wall slides. Avoid lat-dominant programs (pull-ups, bench, heavy deadlifts, farmer's walks, walking DB lunges, all coached lat-dominant). (*5 Reasons for the Increase in Lat Strains*, 2018)
10. **Weighted-ball misuse:** off a mound, in long toss, playing catch, before a bullpen, over 8 oz on lay-back drills, or with unprepared young kids. (2009; 2018)
11. **No year-round throwing.** Short breaks don't count as time off. (2011)
12. **No SS or catcher duty in the days after a 60+ pitch start.** Regular HS pitchers should avoid C/SS/3B. (2014; 2011)
13. **No off-season showcases. No fall ball for 15–16-year-olds.** Don't let ages 9–14 exceed 100 innings a year. (2011)
14. **Don't advance youth (13–15) to med ball steps 3–5. Don't use heavy med balls** for rotational work (above 8 lb, "very rarely" thrown fast). (2018; 2021)
15. **Never train through a shrugging pattern overhead.** (2009)
16. **Don't squat catchers in-season.** (2011)
17. **Don't train the obliques with sit-ups, crunches, or side bends.** (2011)
18. **Don't let the arm drift into horizontal abduction during ER drills** (posterior deltoid). (2018)
19. **Don't use carries alone as "arm care"** for throwers. (2015)
20. **Post-spondylolysis athletes:** EC hasn't returned them to squatting until at least 9 months post-bracing, and no Olympic lifts or straight-bar deadlifts in the first year post-bracing. He tests axial load with a front-squat-grip barbell reverse lunge first. (*Spondylolysis and Young Athletes*, 2008)

## 5. Gaps / weak evidence / where his views changed

### Dated disagreements (report both)
- **Sleeper stretch.**
  - 2007-07-03, *Baseball: Shoulder Strengthening* (https://ericcressey.com/baseball-shoulder-strengthening/). Quote: "if you aren't including this stretch in your program, you shouldn't be allowed to train overhead athletes!"
  - 2008-12-17, *Maximum Strength for Baseball* (https://ericcressey.com/maximum-strength-for-baseball/): "Really emphasize the sleeper stretch."
  - 2009-11: a 10° IR deficit is normal; look at total motion.
  - 2009-12: he prefers the cross-body stretch.
  - 2010-06: "be careful about universally recommending sleeper stretches."
  - **Current-practice reading:** assess first; cross-body over sleeper; stability for lax athletes.
- **Overhead lifting.**
  - 2009 (*Strength Training for Pitchers*): overhead lifting other than chin-ups listed as contraindicated.
  - 2012 (*5 Things…*): "we don't like overhead pressing."
  - 2014-03-28, *How to Build Back to Overhead Pressing* (https://ericcressey.com/build-back-to-overhead-pressing/): general-audience criteria.
  - 2017-12-23, *Should We Really Contraindicate ALL Overhead Lifting?* (https://ericcressey.com/should-we-really-contraindicate-all-overhead-lifting/): he says the "no overhead" stance was "falsely attributed" to him. Quote: "I'm not against overhead lifting." He describes a continuum used with baseball players: bottoms-up KB press, landmine press, bottoms-up carries and waiter's walks, yoga push-ups, TRX Y's, pull-ups, overhead med ball. He still doesn't love heavy barbell or DB overhead pressing for baseball.
  - **Reading:** overhead *reaching and stability* is fine and progressed; heavy overhead *pressing* stays out for most throwers.
- **Cuff work frequency.**
  - 2010: about 4 days/week in the off-season.
  - 2011 in-season and 2012 off-season: 2 days of rhythmic stabs + 2 days of manual resistance.
  - 2018: near-daily small doses (5–6 days/week for pros).
  - Total dose looks similar; the distribution moved toward more frequent, lighter exposures.
- **Early off-season power "deload."**
  - 2011 and 2012: a few weeks off sprinting; some athletes skip med ball for the first month.
  - 2014: Cishek did no aggressive med ball in October.
  - 2021-12 (*Programming Principles 6*). Quote: "I've gotten away from a true month-long deload from sprinting/jumping/aggressive med ball work at the start of the offseason." He now restarts light power work right away.
  - **Reading:** start low-volume power work early, but keep aggressive throwing-specific med ball volume low in month 1 for high-inning arms.
- **In-season med ball:** 2011 says little, and only opposite-direction. The 2021 "can be trained frequently" comment is general (soreness), not an in-season prescription. Not a true conflict.

### Evidence he cites vs. opinion
- **He cites research for:**
  - Post-pitching ROM loss (Reinold et al.).
  - UCL mechanics: UCL takes about 54% of a 64 Nm valgus load, and fails at about 32 Nm in cadavers (Fleisig, Werner, Morrey, as relayed in *Understanding Elbow Pain – Part 3: Throwing Injuries*, 2010-05-17, https://ericcressey.com/understanding-elbow-pain-part-3-throwing-injuries/).
  - Youth overuse (Olsen et al., as relayed in the 2011 coaches post).
  - Resistance training → velocity (DeRenne 2001 review).
  - Plane-specific power (Rhea et al. 2008; McCarthy et al. 1995).
  - Isometric angle carryover.
  - Labral abnormalities in asymptomatic pro pitchers (79%, cited in his labrum materials).
- **Opinion and experience:**
  - The weekly templates.
  - Med ball volumes and loads.
  - Weighted-ball dosing (7–11 oz, set structure).
  - Compression-throw spacing.
  - The tight/loose warm-up model.
  - The 1.5× body-weight deadlift claim.
  - Most velocity anecdotes (client case studies).
- **Counter-evidence on his own site:** Rob Rabena's D-II thesis (guest post, 2012) found **no statistically significant differences** in strength/power markers between interval-sprint and distance-running groups; only a soreness trend (p = .071). The argument against distance running there rests partly on mechanism and preference, not a clear outcome difference.
- **Lehman lateral-jump finding:** correlational, small sample (42), not an intervention study.

### Where free content is thin (would need paid products or audio)
- **Exact arm care exercise libraries with sets/reps** are mostly in video posts or paid products:
  - Video posts: *Optimizing and Progressing Arm Care*, 2017-08-26 (https://ericcressey.com/optimizing-and-progressing-arm-care/); *Elbow-Supported DB External Rotations: Do or Don't?*, 2023-07-19 (https://ericcressey.com/elbow-supported-dumbbell-external-rotations-do-or-dont/); *Prone External Rotation Compensation Patterns*, 2024-05-28 (https://ericcressey.com/prone-external-rotation-compensation-patterns-2/). The prone ER post's text notes athletes substitute elbow flexion or extension near 90° ER: slow down, or switch to a standing version using a rack edge or doorway.
  - Paid products (out of scope): Sturdy Shoulder Solutions, Optimal Shoulder Performance, Assess and Correct, Functional Stability Training, Medicine Ball Master Class, High Performance Handbook, the Elite Baseball Mentorships.
- **His post-throwing stretch list** is offered behind a newsletter sign-up (*Year-Round Throwing Part 2*). Not retrieved.
- **2025–26 podcast episodes** (weighted balls and injury, Little League elbow, youth strength and injury risk, scapular posterior tilt; show notes for episodes #36, #43, #49, #54, #78, #90) have only one-line summaries plus sponsor copy. No transcripts. His *current* positions on these topics are not recoverable from free text.
- **No free, dosed throwing program** (interval or return-to-throw) and no youth throwing progression from EC himself.
- **Position-player and catcher arm care** is thin. Catchers are covered mainly by a guest post: Dan Rosen, *4 Training Considerations for Catchers*, 2022-10-11 (https://ericcressey.com/4-training-considerations-for-catchers/), skimmed only.
- **Elbow/flexor-pronator strengthening:** discussed conceptually (UCL protection via forearm musculature, ROM restoration) but no dosed protocol was found in free text.
- **AC joint:** mentioned only in passing in *Shoulder Hurts? Start Here*, EC, 2011-05-16 (https://ericcressey.com/shoulder-hurts-start-here/). No baseball-specific AC protocol.
- **Guest posts:** several of the most HS-practical posts are written by CSP staff (O'Neil, Blake, Bonvechio, Kraus, Cerbie, Schoenberg), not EC.

## Handoff: top 12 implementables for Strength Coach

1. **Intake screen for every thrower:**
   - Total-motion IR+ER (both sides).
   - Beighton score.
   - Resting scap position.
   - Supine shoulder flexion.
   - Seated hip IR.
   - Lumbar-locked T-spine rotation.
   - Then classify each athlete tight / loose / middle.
2. **Warm up to throw, 15–24 min, by type:**
   - Tight: length, then activation.
   - Loose: no stretching; standing work, prone ER, rhythmic stabs.
3. **Cuff = strength + timing:**
   - Drills: manual-resistance ER, rhythmic stabs, 90/90 holds, ball-release-position stabs.
   - Dose: near-daily small doses.
   - Never to failure, and never a heavy circuit right before throwing.
4. **Within 24 h of every outing:** restore shoulder IR and elbow extension. Cross-body stretch only if indicated. No flush runs.
5. **In-season structure:**
   - Starters: 7-day template.
   - Relievers: Mon/Thu lifts at reduced volume.
   - Position players: 2× full-body per week.
   - Sessions ≤35–40 min, 10–14 sets. No new exercises.
6. **Conditioning:** sprints ≤60 yd at 90%+, 2×/week (max 3). No distance running (anything over 150 m counts).
7. **Med ball:**
   - Use the 5-step rotational progression. Ages 13–15 stay at steps 1–2.
   - 4–8 lb balls.
   - Cues: head behind belly button; slingshot legs; hand to the wall.
   - High volume in the off-season; low volume and non-dominant direction in-season.
8. **Lifting menu:**
   - Use: deadlift variations, front or safety-bar squat, single-leg work, rows and chins (≤1×/week vertical pull in-season), push-ups and DB/neutral-grip pressing, landmine and bottoms-up KB for overhead work.
   - Avoid: straight-bar bench, upright rows, raises, Olympic lifts, heavy overhead pressing.
9. **T-spine:** arm-independent drills early in the off-season and in-season; windmills in months 3–4; no KB arm bar.
10. **Scapula:** coach scapular *movement* (upward rotation). Drop blanket "down and back." Watch for lat creep and posterior-deltoid drift in ER work.
11. **Workload and calendar:**
    - At least 2 continuous months a year with no throwing.
    - HS innings about 80–100 max.
    - No SS or catcher duty after a 60+ pitch start.
    - No fall ball or off-season showcases for underclassmen.
    - Cut arm care, med ball, and upper lifting as throwing volume rises.
12. **Throwing tools:**
    - Long toss: compression throws on the way in, on a straight line.
    - Weighted balls: advanced athletes only, net throws, 7–11 oz finishing at 5 oz, never before a pen, never off a mound.
