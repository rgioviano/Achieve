## Holík, Krejčí, De Ste Croix, Lehnert (2026). PLoS One 21(5): e0349011. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0349011
Design: 12-wk RCT, 37 trained male youth soccer players (10–18 y) split pre-/post-PHV (Mirwald), plyo 2x/wk ~20 min in-season, progressing slow SSC (>250 ms) to fast SSC (<250 ms), multidirectional, mostly submaximal hops/rebounds.

Decision it changes: whether post-PHV high schoolers keep a plyo block in-season, and which plyo type to pick for the quality we want.
Who: youth and serious high school athletes, especially post-PHV.
Findings: plyo improved 20-m sprint (d -0.77 pre, -0.55 post), broad jump (d ~0.6), single-leg triple jump, and relative leg stiffness (d 0.81 / 0.72). Post-PHV controls without plyo LOST leg stiffness (d -0.58) and stalled on sprint. CMJ and drop-jump RSI did not change in any group.
Change: keep 2x/wk short plyo (~20 min, >=48 h apart) year-round for post-PHV athletes, including in-season and 1x/wk step-downs where possible. Submaximal rebound hops build stiffness; if the goal is RSI, add maximal-effort drop/rebound jumps with minimal contact time; if the goal is CMJ, use larger-ROM hip/knee jumps.
Don't change: maturity doesn't change WHETHER to plyo (no maturity interaction). Pre-PHV kids improve partly from growth alone.
Testing: CMJ height missed changes that broad jump, triple hop, and 2.5 Hz hopping stiffness picked up. Don't judge a fast-SSC/stiffness block by CMJ height alone.
Cue/number: post-PHV with no plyo lost stiffness in 12 weeks (d -0.58).
Limits: small (n 7–10 per cell), single soccer club, boys only, no a-priori power; maturity offset ±0.5 y. Inference that this transfers to swimmers is unproven (swim has little ground contact) — treat as land-sport/HS athlete evidence.
