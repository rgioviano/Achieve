## 1. Core theory / asymmetry model (this course)

Myokinematic Restoration owns **acetabulum-on-femur (AF) and femur-on-acetabulum (FA)** pathomechanics of the Left AIC pattern - how the pelvis orients the hip socket and how the femur orients in that socket across stance/swing.

### Stance / pattern definitions (manual)
- **Left stance in Left AF IR = Right AIC pattern** (desired alternating opposite)  
- **Right stance in Right AF IR = Left AIC pattern** (typical compensatory default)  
- **Right stance in Left AF ER = Left AIC pattern** (same family)

**Desirable "real estate" for repositioning:** maximize **Left AF IR & Right AF ER** and motor control in that orientation. Left AF ER / Right AF IR is the patterned Left AIC state to leave.

### Why Left AIC dominates lumbo-pelvic-femoral findings
Same systemic asymmetry as other intros (right hemi-diaphragm + liver, right stance bias). Left pelvis anterior/forward → femur biomechanically oriented; psoas fails as femoral ER; VL and biceps femoris become antagonistic stabilizers (shared AIC description across manuals). Symptoms can present at knee, hip, groin, SI, back, shoulder blade, neck, face/TMJ with inability to fully adduct/extend/flex one or both sides and limited trunk rotation.

### Positional influences (themes from manual sections)
Patients stuck in **right AF IR + left AF ER** show characteristic length/strength imbalances:
- Posterior glute med (ER): long/weak secondary to adduction + IR  
- Anterior glute med (IR): long secondary to adduction but can be short/strong positionally  
- Hamstrings: proximal attachment more forward/elevated secondary to anterior innominate rotation → SLR looks "tight" while group is long, hypotonic, cortically suppressed  
- Right adductors / FA IRs overactive; femoral head can impinge anterior-medial acetabular cotyloid rim on FA ER secondary to right AF IR osseous position  

### Soft tissue / capsule after repositioning
If capsular/soft-tissue issues remain after pelvic repositioning (over-compensatory activity):
- At 90° flexion, IR can be checked by superior ischiofemoral ligament and/or inferior capsule  
- At 90° flexion, ER can be checked by iliofemoral ligament and/or superior capsule  
Manual pairs stretches + AF/FA muscle strategies (IC adductor approximation, AF ER glute max, anterior/posterior glute med, obturator foramen control) by which rotation is limited left vs right.

## 2. Patterns to recognize

### Non-patho compensatory Left AIC (Myokinematic test results table)
| PRI test | Typical Left AIC result |
|---|---|
| Adduction Drop Test | + Left (unable to adduct left) |
| Extension Drop Test | often related findings; + L EDT with + L ADT relationships discussed |
| Straight Leg Raise | ↓ Left SLR |
| Trunk Rotation | Limited right trunk rotation (legs/rotation toward left limited in the patterned sense described) |
| FA ROM | ↓ Left FA IR ROM; ↓ Right FA ER ROM (vs opposite) |
| Hruska Adduction Lift | Level findings (often limited early levels on patterned side) |
| Hruska Abduction Lift | Level findings (e.g. Level 2 right noted in table OCR) |

### Functional relationships (manual shorthand)
- + L ADT with + L EDT relationships; − L EDT ↔ iliofemoral/pubofemoral laxity themes  
- Limited R trunk rotation vs non-limited (right iliolumbar laxity note)  
- ↓ L SLR = long, neuromuscularly weak, hypotonic, overly stretched "inactive" hamstring group  
- ↓ L FA IR ROM secondary to left AF ER osseous position  
- ↓ R FA ER ROM secondary to overactive right adductors/FA IRs + anterior-medial rim impingement in right AF IR  
- + R ADT with + L ADT → think **PEC** (hand off toward Pelvis / Postural Respiration)  
- L AF IR = R AF ER ↔ Right AIC (desired); R AF IR = L AF ER ↔ Left AIC (patterned)

### Femoral Acetabular ROM (seated) interpretation
Seated FA IR/ER with hips/knees ~90° (vs prone) to keep ligamentous site more resting. In non-pathologic compensatory Left AIC:
- ↓ L FA IR may reflect abducted left femur in L AF ER when legs face examiner → femur hits posterior-inferior acetabular rim on FA IR  
- ↓ R FA ER may reflect adducted right femur in R AF IR → femur "impinges" anterior-superior-medial acetabular rim on FA ER  

**After successful repositioning without osseous/capsular block:** expect ↑ L FA IR and ↑ R FA ER toward normal vs pre-repositioning. If still restricted → soft-tissue pathology pathway (not just "more stretching blindly").

### FA neuromuscular control
Seated FA IR/ER strength; note **which muscle the patient feels on FA IR strength** (glute med vs TFL). TFL dominance = need repositioning / different facilitator strategy (manual testing note).

## 3. Assessment / special tests (this course)

### Core battery (evaluation form + test chapters)
1. **Adduction Drop Test (ADT)** - position / Left AIC flag  
2. **Extension Drop Test (EDT)** - hip ligamentous laxity / extension pathomechanics  
3. **Trunk Rotation**  
4. **Straight Leg Raise (SLR)**  
5. **FA IR / FA ER ROM** (goniometric, seated)  
6. **FA IR / FA ER strength** (+ which muscle felt on IR)  
7. **Hruska Adduction Lift Test**  
8. **Hruska Abduction Lift Test**  

Evaluation form also lists Pelvis / Postural / Cervical / Advanced Integration blocks - Myokinematic sits first for AF/FA.

### ADT
Same sidelying procedure as other courses. Positive usually left in Left AIC; bilateral → PEC family.

### Hruska Adduction Lift Test (named for leg on examiner's shoulder)
Example: patient left sidelying, right ankle on examiner's shoulder → **Right** Hruska Adduction Lift.  
Position: back rounded; uppermost LE on shoulder (neutral hip, extended knee); lower leg flexed; pelvis neutral (no forward/back upper-pelvis rotation).  
Mechanics (stop at failed step):  
1. Raise ankle of flexed lower leg to upper knee  
2. Raise flexed lower knee while keeping ankle to knee  
3. Raise lower hip maintaining above  
Grading themes: early failure ↔ FA ER weakness or AF stability failure of active extremity; higher levels demand IC adductor / obturator / pelvic diaphragm / oblique integration (OCR levels 1–5).

### Hruska Abduction Lift Test
Named for leg on wall (e.g. left sidelying, right foot on wall → Right test). Used as myokinematic measurement of muscle position, strength, timing.  
Steps (stop at failure): press bottom toes to wall → press bottom hip into surface (lower abs/adductors) → turn/raise bottom knee inward (IC adductor + anterior glute med) without top hip moving back → turn top heel up / toes down without dropping bottom knee → raise top leg off wall with trunk/hips stabilized.  
Also used in **Pelvis Restoration** algorithms as a progression gate (e.g. 3/5 before upright frontal-plane control).

## 4. Inhibit vs facilitate (Myokinematic composite)

### General AIC (Myokinematic column)
**Facilitate:** hamstrings, ischiocondylar adductor, gluteus maximus, anterior glute med/min, posterior glute med/min, IO/TA  
**Inhibit:** TFL, adductor magnus, psoas major, piriformis, vastus lateralis  

### As related to Left AIC / Right BC / Right TMCC
**Facilitate:** L AF hamstrings, L AF IC adductor, R AF glute max, L FA anterior glute med/min, R FA posterior glute med/min, L IO/TA  
**Inhibit:** L TFL, R FA adductor magnus, L psoas major, L FA piriformis, R AF piriformis, R vastus lateralis  

Muscles that pull **femur into acetabulum** vs **acetabulum onto femur** are distinguished (glute max vs glute med/min themes) - AF IR work is not "more clamshells."

## 5. Repositioning logic and exercise progressions

### Course-selection gate (from Pelvis Identification page - critical)
After Left AIC repositioning attempt:
- **− L ADT / − L PADT → stay in Myokinematic Restoration Program**  
- **− L ADT / + L PADT → Pelvis Restoration Program**  

Bilateral:
- **− B ADT / − B PADT → Myokinematic + Postural Respiration**  
- **− B ADT / + B PADT → Pelvis Restoration**  

### Left AIC repositioning menu (clean cues from Pelvis appendix reprint of Myokinematic techniques)

**1. 90-90 Supported Hip Lift with Hemibridge**  
Feet on wall, hips/knees 90°. Exhale into pelvic tilt (tailbone slightly off; low back flat). Dig heels (not flat feet). Maintain tilt on left; straighten right; slowly on/off wall with breath - feel left posterior thigh. 3×10.

**2. Right Sidelying Respiratory Left Adductor Pull Back**  
Right sidelying, feet on wall, hips/knees 90°, ankles/knees together, back rounded. Bolster between feet; towel between knees; left knee lower than left hip/ankle. Push right foot into wall. Inhale - pull left leg back; exhale - squeeze left knee into towel 3s. Repeat 4–5 breaths, pulling further each inhale. ×5 sequences.

**3. Left Sidelying Right Glute Max**  
Left sidelying, feet on wall, hips/knees 90°, back rounded. Towel under ankles and left abs. Press left hip down (right hip toward wall) - feel left abs. Shift right hip forward to slight stretch left outer hip. Turn right knee out using wall as fulcrum without right hip moving back - feel right outer hip. 4–5 breaths. ×5.

### Hierarchy themes (agenda: Left AIC Myokinematic Hierarchy; Femoroacetabular / AF IR programs)
Progress from repositioning → FA/AF ROM & strength restoration → Hruska lift level improvements → upright Left AF IR / Right AF ER integration (standing wall-supported left knee flexion with resisted right glute max; all-four right glute max; standing resisted patterns; lateral walking integration; right adductor magnus inhibition in hooklying; left posterior capsule inhibition standing; QL/psoas inhibition pairs).

**Right piriformis syndrome considerations** (manual section): Right Sidelying Respiratory Resisted Left Adductor Pull Back; sidelying obturator/reciprocal techniques; standing wall-supported ischial femoral ligamentous stretch; seated supported Left AF IR with right iliacus and right FA ER; retro stairs with resisted glute max - as listed on that considerations page.

### Inhibition examples (appendix titles)
- Supine Hooklying Adductor Magnus Inhibition (right) - pelvic tilt + allow right knee to bolster; breathing holds  
- Left Posterior Capsule Inhibition - standing variants  
- Right QL and Left Psoas Inhibition - supine banded variants

## 6. What NOT to do / common errors

- Do **not** stretch "tight hamstrings" as the primary fix when ↓ SLR reflects anterior innominate / long inhibited hamstrings.  
- Do **not** strengthen TFL-dominant FA IR - note which muscle is felt; reposition and facilitate glute med/IC adductor pathways.  
- Do **not** chase FA ER on the right with brute force while still in Right AF IR osseous position (rim impingement risk).  
- Do **not** keep someone in Myokinematic-only work when **PADT stays +** after − ADT - that is Pelvis Restoration.  
- Do **not** skip hemibridge / adductor pull-back / right glute max repositioning before advanced Hruska lift upright integration.  
- Do **not** interpret bilateral + ADT as "two unilateral Left AIC programs" only - PEC path.  
- Hruska lift compensations (flexing contralateral FA, pressing too hard into examiner's shoulder, lumbar extension instead of FA extension) invalidate the grade - coach the test, don't inflate the score.
