## 1. Core theory / asymmetry model (this course)

### Why the thorax is patterned
Humans are inherently asymmetrical: larger/stronger right hemi-diaphragm, liver on the right (better positions the right diaphragm), no liver on the left → tendency to relax the left abdominal wall. Early development biases shift/rotation of the spine to the right. Right extremity dominance and daily right weight-shift reinforce this.

The pattern **most often prevalent** (manual Section: Left AIC pattern):
- **Left Anterior Interior Chain (Left AIC)**
- **Right Brachial Chain (Right BC)**
- **Right posterior back muscles (PEC contribution)**

Left pelvis anteriorly tipped and forwardly rotated → directional rotational influence on low back/spine to the right → compulsive compensatory movement in trunk, UEs, and cervical-cranial-mandibular musculature. **Greatest impact is on rib alignment and position → breathing.**

Common objective findings secondary to trying to stay balanced over that unlevel pelvis (manual):
- Elevated anterior ribs on the left
- Lowered/depressed shoulder and chest on the right
- Posterior rib hump on the right
- Overdeveloped lower right back muscle
- Spinal curvature; head/face asymmetry
- One shoulder lower; continual shift of body to one side through the hips
- Difficulty adducting/extending/flexing legs one or both sides; limited trunk rotation one or both ways; limited apical expansion one or both sides; cervical rotation / mandibular / shoulder flexion / horizontal abduction / IR limitations

Respiratory dysfunction (e.g. asthma) and occupational/repetitive positions can also drive compensatory left anterior/forward pelvis.

### Polyarticular chains (Postural Respiration definitions)

**Anterior Interior Chain (AIC)**  
Two AIC tracts (L and R). Attachments from costal cartilage/bone of ribs 7–12 to lateral patella, fibular head, lateral tibial condyle. Core members: **diaphragm + psoas**, with iliacus, TFL, biceps femoris, vastus lateralis. Influence respiration, trunk/ribcage/spine rotation, and LE position.

Suboptimal AIC mechanics (manual AIC section):
1. Diaphragm pulls dorsal (thoracic) spine up and forward  
2. Psoas + iliacus pull lumbar spine and pelvis down and forward  
3. Combined → increased lumbar lordosis (hemi-lordosis), anterior pelvic tilt, active lumbar extension / hyperinflation  
4. Diaphragm becomes more tonic/postural → less effective as a respiratory muscle; thorax form/function challenged  
5. Posterior mediastinal expansion often restricted  
6. Ilium anterior + forward → femur oriented inward; psoas fails as femoral ER; VL and biceps femoris become antagonistic hip stabilizers

**Brachial Chain (BC)**  
Two BC tracts over anterior pleural/cervical area. Influence cervical rotation, shoulder dynamics, apical inspiratory expansion. Attach from costal cartilages/bone of ribs 4–7 and xiphoid to occipital bone, mandible, coracoid. Members: **triangularis sterni, SCM, scalenes, pec minor, intercostals, pharynx/anterior neck**.  
- Right BC opposed by right PEC, lower trap, serratus, external rib rotators, **plus left internal obliques**  
- Left BC opposed by left PEC, lower trap, serratus, external rib rotators, **plus right internal obliques**

**Posterior Exterior Chain (PEC)**  
Posterior extensors / QL system that closes the posterior mediastinum and drives bilateral extension bias when both sides are "on."

### Zone of Apposition (ZOA) - central concept of this course
ZOA = cylindrical portion of diaphragm apposed to inner aspect of lower rib cage (from costal insertion up to costophrenic angle where fibers leave the rib cage to form the free dome). In standing quiet breathing ≈ **¼–⅓ of total rib cage surface**; at rest overall ≈ **~30%**. Controlled by abdomen/obliques; directs diaphragmatic tension. Rib cage and abdomen are always mechanically coupled through ZOA.

- Elevated anterior, externally rotated ribs → **decreased ZOA** (uni- or bilateral) depending on pattern, abdominal weakness, and use  
- Chronic hyperinflation changes diaphragm dimensions **exclusively in the ZOA** (manual cites)  
- Loss of ipsilateral/bilateral abdominal opposition → hyperinflation, paradoxical breathing, accessory overuse  

**ZOA restoration logic (Hruska):**  
Apposition lost almost always on the **left** (→ Left AIC) or **bilaterally** (→ PEC). IO/TA primarily responsible for ipsilateral diaphragm leaflet opposition and ipsilateral lower rib opposition on inspiration → contralateral apical expansion (esp. in trunk rotation/gait).

Active established ZOA example: successful **standing reach (fingers to toes)** while inhaling with **anterior mediastinal compression + posterior mediastinal expansion**. Passive ZOA via PRI manual techniques if active ZOA unobtainable. Maximum ZOA (passive, supine): end-exhale trans-diaphragmatic strength strongest in thoraco-lumbar flexion, weakest in extension.

Reference centers emphasized on Integration techniques (CD notes): **left abdominals, left ischial seat, left heel, right arch**.

### Posterior mediastinum (Right BC context)
Mnemonic **DATES**: Descending aorta, Azygous/hemiazygous, Thoracic duct, Esophagus, Sympathetic trunk. Bounded by pericardium (ant), diaphragm (inf), transverse thoracic plane T4 (sup), vertebral column (post), pleura (lat).  
- Unlocks transverse plane when **opened (flexed)**; limits thoracic rotation when **closed (extended)** in PEC  
- In Right BC: more **closed/anterior on the left**, more **opened/posterior on the right**

## 2. Patterns to recognize (with this course's lens)

### Left AIC (thoraco-abdominal expression)
Left hemi-pelvis anterior/forward; mass/orientation to the right; left ZOA lost; left anterior ribs elevated; airflow often easier into left upper chest with compensatory strategies. Compels Right BC above the diaphragm to keep the person facing forward.

### Right BC
Compensatory upper thorax/rib cage orientation (often described as thorax rotating back toward left above diaphragm for frontal-plane balance and right UE reach). Right low trap, triceps, serratus lose normal scapular retraction / spinal stabilization / rib IR–ER roles because of right-handed activity + left upper trunk rotation.

Manual Right BC upright scapular pattern cues (visual): forward rib cage with possible rib hump; humeral IR in the patterned position can drive **posterior-superior humeral glenoid impingement** (link to Impingement & Instability).

### Bilateral PEC
Bilateral extension / hyperinflation expression. Bilateral limitations on key tests (below). Posterior mediastinum closed; stiff/rigid back; limited bilateral arm swing; limited apical expansion one or both sides.

### Combined Left AIC / Right BC / Right TMCC
Composite tables in the manual treat these as the typical coupled system. Cervical Revolution covers Right TMCC in depth; Postural Respiration still expects you to know the thorax piece couples into cervical-cranial patterns.

1. *Apical Expansion  
2. *Shoulder Horizontal Abduction  
3. Trunk Rotation  
4. *Posterior Mediastinum Respiratory Reach Test  
5. Humeral Glenoid Internal Rotation  
6. Shoulder Flexion  
7. Cervical Axial Rotation  

**BC opposition muscles:** triceps, lower trapezius, abdominal obliques, TA, serratus anterior.

### PEC tests (* = key)
1. *Trunk Rotation (bilateral limitation)  
2. *Adduction Drop Test (bilateral limitation)  
3. *Extension Drop Test (bilateral limitation)  
4. Apical Expansion  
5. Posterior Mediastinum Respiratory Reach Test  

**PEC opposition:** abdominal obliques.

### Adduction Drop Test (procedure + meaning)
Sidelying, bottom hip/knee flexed ~90°. Passively flex–abduct–extend top hip to neutral keeping ~90° knee flexion; stabilize pelvis so it does not fall back / allow femoral IR. Top innominate directly over bottom (too cephalad → false +; too caudal → false −).  
**Positive:** femur will not adduct (restriction from anterior-inferior acetabular labral rim, transverse ligament, piriformis, or posterior-inferior femoral neck on posterior-inferior acetabular rim) - often secondary to anteriorly rotated, forward hemipelvis. **Usually left in Left AIC.**

### Standing reach / active ZOA screen
Inability to touch toes with posterior expansion on inhale = ZOA / extension-bias problem (ties to PEC and hyperinflation). Successful toe touch **with** anterior compression + posterior expansion on inhale ≈ active ZOA.

### Right BC treatment-guideline test cluster (appendix)
Track as positive/negative:
- Left horizontal abduction  
- Left cervical axial rotation  
- Left humeral glenoid flexion  
- Right apical expansion  
- Right humeral glenoid IR

## 5. Repositioning logic and non-manual progressions

### Order philosophy (PRI Non-Manual CD organization - Postural Respiration)
Typical L AIC + R BC involves pelvic/spinal/diaphragmatic orientation toward the right with thorax rotating back left above the diaphragm. Right-handed activity promotes that left upper trunk rotation and disadvantages R low trap / triceps / serratus.

**Neuromotor order for this course's muscle programs:**
1. **Right low trap + right tricep** - retract/posterior-tilt scapula; lower spinal rotation left; promote thoracic flexion; deactivate lats  
2. **Left low trap + left serratus** - mid-spinal rotation right; stabilize thorax for right thoracic rotation and left rib IR; serratus lower fibers ER ribs / upper fibers IR ribs; promote desired kyphosis; engage serratus via arms reaching forward **and** ribs moving back  
3. **Right serratus** - stabilize right scapula for right arm reach **without** left trunk rotation / right rib retraction  
4. **Right subscapularis** - true HG IR independent of lat (after neutrality)

**Position progressions (neuro-motor demand increases):**  
- R low trap/tricep: Supine → All Four → Seated → Long Seated → Standing → Sidelying → Prone  
- L serratus: Supine → Sidelying → Seated → Standing → All Four  
- L low trap: Supine → All Four → Seated → Standing → Sidelying  
- R serratus: Supine → Sidelying → Seated → Long Seated → Standing → All Four  
- R subscap: Supine → Sidelying → Seated → Prone → Standing  

Use these **after** repositioning with a non-manual or manual technique. Later positions demand more isolation with greater inhibition of substitutions.

**Inhibition program rule:** bilateral inhibition precedes unilateral. Terminology: *Respiratory* = expands left posterior mediastinum / left posterior outlet / left posterior capsule; *Reciprocal* = full ROM both directions; *Alternating* = one side then the other; *Supported* / *Resisted* / *Weighted* as defined in CD notes.

- All tests − **and** sufficient R ST function (can reach in supine/prone/left sidelying without tests returning +) → **Standing** R low trap / R tricep.

**Right BC inhibition considerations** if: limited R apical expansion; limited L shoulder horizontal abduction; difficulty with deep inspiration without compensation (L ribs flare, L back extends, R SCM, lateralize body right); ongoing tension R upper trap / SCM / QL / lat / paraspinals / hip flexors. - R Sidelying R Apical Expansion with Balloon

## 6. What NOT to do / common errors

- Do **not** load heavy sagittal strength on a flared, extended, ZOA-less thorax and call breathing "done."  
- Do **not** chase "weak serratus" or "tight shoulder" before asking what the **ribcage under the scapula** is doing (winging often = rib position).  
- Do **not** train true HG IR via lat-dominated patterns when Right BC / lat is the problem - restore thorax neutrality then train **subscapularis**.  
- Do **not** skip the neuromotor order (R low trap/tricep before L low trap/serratus before R serratus before R subscap) without a reason.  
- Do **not** use late-position progressions (sidelying/prone standing variants) before the person can demonstrate the acquired action with less inhibitory demand.  
- Do **not** treat bilateral PEC like unilateral Left AIC only - need bilateral ADT/EDT/trunk rotation logic and paravertebral/reach-based inhibition.  
- Manual Superior T4 / Subclavius / Infraclavicular pathways are clinician tools; S&C should not improvise them.  
- CD note: if a technique seems "in the wrong muscle program," assume intentional neuromotor design - don't reshuffle by bodybuilding logic.

## 8. Achieve programming translation (S&C)

- Early session: ZOA/repositioning (90-90 balloon family, wall-supported reach, sidelying adductor/glute patterns as indicated).  
- Swimmers / overhead: bias lat inhibition, serratus + subscap, wall reach / squat-reach, avoid feeding bilateral PEC extension.  
- Pair extension-biased strength with flexion/reach or exhalation-biased breathing check.  
- Gate "earned" inhale-on-descent squat breathing until ZOA is demonstrated.  
- Use test language in coach notes (ADT, apical expansion, trunk rotation, HG IR) without claiming clinical diagnosis.
