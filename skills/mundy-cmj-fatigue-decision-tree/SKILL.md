# Mundy CMJ fatigue decision tree

**Source:** Peter Mundy (@drpetermundy) Instagram — CMJ fatigue monitoring framework using the FTO model (Sep 2026). Eight steps from one jump to one decision. He was purposefully vague on numeric thresholds; they are **population-specific**. Ask for local norms rather than copying universal cutoffs.

## Decision stance

1. Start from **one quality CMJ** (or session-best under a fixed protocol), not a noisy mean of mixed intents.
2. Walk a short decision tree: outcome → phase strategy → fatigue vs adaptation vs noise → action.
3. **Thresholds are population-specific.** Do not invent % drops or RSI cutoffs Mundy did not publish. Use your sport/age/sex norms, or flag “needs local baseline” when none exist.
4. **Asymmetry is a separate flag.** Do not bury L/R inside the main fatigue tree; call it out on its own and act only when the asymmetry is real, reliable, and decision-relevant.
5. **Reliability is a condition of use.** If the metric is too noisy for this athlete or this setup, do not drive a training decision from it — even if it sits in the FTO set.

## Multi-domain context (optional)

Rebelo et al. (2026), *Monitoring training effects in athletes: a multidimensional framework for decision making* (Sports Med 56:1603–1624), as cited on the account: neuromuscular (plates) sits beside external load, internal load, autonomic/cardiovascular, biochemical/hormonal, and psychological/social/sleep. A CMJ dip alone is not a full readiness call when other domains disagree.

## How to use in a brief

1. State the CMJ change with date, n, units, and which method/field (height method continuity matters).
2. Say whether the change looks like strategy shift, capacity drop, or possible noise — using phase metrics you actually have.
3. Apply **local** thresholds only; if none, recommend collecting a stable baseline before hard cuts.
4. Report asymmetry separately (direction, %, which phase/impulse) or omit if not on the test.
5. If load, sleep, or illness context is known, one sentence of domain context — do not invent it.

## What this skill does not do

- Publish universal fatigue cutoffs.
- Replace medical or return-to-play clearance.
- Collapse asymmetry into “fatigued / not fatigued.”
