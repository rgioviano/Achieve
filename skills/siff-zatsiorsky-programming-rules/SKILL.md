# Siff + Zatsiorsky programming rules (Ryne-approved 2026-10-05)

Full reference with page refs: `/workspace/books/strength/strength-key-ideas.md`. Achieve trains athletes in the gym only. There's no pool training, so skip pool-based tools (drag suits, paddles, in-water work).

## Approved rules

1. **Check the explosive strength deficit (ESD) before picking the block emphasis.** ESD is how much of an athlete's max strength they can't express in a fast sport movement: ESD % = 100 x (max force - force in the sport action) / max force. If ESD is well under 50%, more max strength still pays. Around 50% or more, shift volume toward rate of force development, speed-strength, and plyos. Zatsiorsky's measure: peak force in the explosive sport action divided against max force in the most favorable slow condition, e.g. peak force on the shot vs bench 1RM per arm (Zat p. 27-28, 116). On Achieve plates the closest proxy is DSI (jump peak force vs isometric peak force). Siff's separate field check is squat jump vs countermovement jump (Siff p. 9-10).
2. **Progress jumps from force plate data, not a fixed calendar.** The general order is ordinary jumps, then barbell strength, then loaded jumps, then depth jumps, but the plates decide when an athlete moves up. Change the main power method when the plates stall, and use roughly 12-18 weeks on one method as the outer limit. (Siff p. 287-289, 295; Zat p. 128)
3. **Gate depth jumps.** Require months of barbell work and ordinary jumps first. Set drop height with the heel test (the highest drop where the heels aren't forced to the ground). No vests or cords. Use them 1-2 times a week, for at most 1-2 blocks in a row. In season, use one exposure every 7-14 days and none in the last 10 days before a key meet. (Siff p. 221, 273-274, 279-280; Zat p. 128-129)
4. **Progress reactive work by raising the drop height, not the load.** Keep ground contact short, like a hot surface, and don't coach stuck landings as reactive training. (Siff p. 221-222, 266; Zat p. 125-128)
5. **Depth jumps go after heavy strength work in the same session, never before.** Resistance work followed by depth jumps beat depth jumps alone. Ordinary jumps and other speed-strength work still go early, before strength work, while the athlete is fresh. (Siff p. 275, 288)
6. **Use the Rule of 60% on accessory volume.** The lightest week or day carries about 60% of the volume of the heaviest, and load fluctuates rather than rising every week. (Siff p. 84, 90-91, 316; Zat p. 97)
7. **Primary lifts can carry over between programs. Change secondary and tertiary exercises every program.** (Zat p. 97, 103, 155-156)
8. **Expect results 2-6 weeks after a hard block (about 4 weeks on average).** Retest after the unload, not at the end of the loading weeks, and don't stack a new hard block before the delayed effect shows. (Zat p. 98, 104; Siff p. 292)
9. **In-season sessions run 35-45 minutes, with 2 short heavy sessions a week to hold strength.** At the end of a taper, right before competition, sessions drop to 35 minutes. Don't drop to moderate-only loading for longer than about a month. (Zat p. 77, 97, 104; Siff p. 316)
13. **For youth under about 14:** no near-max overhead lifts, low-back work at 10 or more reps, no 1RM testing in the first weeks (estimate it from a 5-6RM), and at least 2 sessions a week to hold gains. (Zat p. 198-204; Siff p. 323-324)
14. **For adults over about 60:** add one power slot at 30-40% 1RM using exercises with no deceleration, keep sets around 3 reps short of failure, and space very heavy days 3-7 or more days apart. (Zat p. 219-225)
15. **Start every new adult client under the Statute of Fitness Limitations.** Lift one limit at a time, based on coach technique rating plus athlete RPE. (Siff p. 428)

## Rejected

- Rules 10 and 11, swim pool tools and timing recovery around pool doubles. Achieve does no pool training.
- Rule 12, weekly readiness triage plus monthly test battery.
