# Mundy CMJ equifinality and strategy read

**Source:** Peter Mundy (@drpetermundy) Instagram force-plate series (Sep 2026). Measurement and decision framework only — he does not mandate fixed lifts from a pattern. Pair with your local curve-shape and exercise language.

## Core idea

Same jump height can hide opposite strategies (**equifinality**). Do not prescribe from jump height or impulse alone. Inspect force, time, velocity, and displacement across the jump.

Classic contrast (same height):
- **Deep countermovement + lower propulsive force + longer propulsive phase**
- **Shallow countermovement + higher propulsive force + shorter propulsive phase**

Train the **force-time strategy**, not height alone. Ask whether the athletes need different emphases, and what else you need (braking quality, phase times, asymmetry, fatigue context).

## Preferred views over composites

1. **Jump height × time to takeoff (TTT)** — quadrant view. Same height with short vs long TTT points to different strategies.
2. **Relative average propulsive force × propulsive time** — more informative than impulse alone. Impulse collapses force and time; the plot keeps both visible.
3. **Components before composites** — composites have a purpose; the phase components are what you train against.

## Physics note

Work-energy and impulse-momentum both describe the same jump (Linthorne 2001, *Analysis of standing vertical jumps using a force platform*). Two valid descriptions of one change in state — not competing “methods” for deciding strategy.

## How to use in a plate brief

1. Report jump height with date and method continuity (do not mix flight-time vs impulse-momentum systems without labeling).
2. Add TTT (or contraction time / equivalent) and say which quadrant the athlete sits in relative to their own baseline or peer group.
3. Prefer **relative** propulsive force (per body mass) over absolute force when comparing athletes; absolute force largely ranks by body mass.
4. Name the strategy in plain words (deep-slow vs shallow-fast, or mixed), then give week interventions that match that strategy and the curve shape — not a height chase.

## What this skill does not do

- Invent thresholds or exercises Mundy did not state.
- Replace curve-shape classification or local exercise progressions.
- Treat impulse or a single composite as a sufficient training target.
