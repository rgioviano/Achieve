# PRI injury and pain presentations (advisor index)

Always classify the pattern first (`eval-cutoffs`, then `left-aic` / `right-bc` / `pec` / `patho-pec`). Pick one named non-manual from the pattern or course list they have earned. Manual techniques stay clinician-only. Do not invent a new ladder because the symptom has a name.

## Across patterns (shared intro language)

People with symptoms at the knee, hip, groin, sacroiliac joint, back, top of the shoulder, between the shoulder blades, neck, face, or TMJ will show inability to fully adduct, extend, or flex the legs on one or both sides, limited trunk rotation, and often limited apical expansion, cervical rotation, mandibular movement, shoulder flexion, horizontal abduction, or IR. (**PRI**, Postural Respiration and Pelvis Restoration, shared introductory symptom language.) That list is a reason to test, not a reason to skip the pattern screen.

## Low back

No dedicated PRI gym ladder. Do not invent one. The 2008 Straub case (*PRI Low Back Pain Case Study*) was classified Left AIC / Right BC, not Patho PEC, despite a 0-inch toe-touch, because ADT was positive on the left only. Visit 1 used the current Left AIC best set (90-90 Hip Lift with Hemibridge; Right Sidelying Left Adductor Pullback; Left Sidelying Right Glute Max). Visits 2-3 were multi-drill clinic sessions - do not copy that volume to the gym. Patho PEC still requires +B ADT and +B PADT.

## SI / ilio-sacral

Pelvis Restoration has a separate **Ilio-Sacral (IS) Instability Treatment Hierarchy** for Left AIC patients - do not skip to aggressive alternating work before inlet/outlet sequencing (`pri-pelvis-restoration`). Myokinematic Restoration appendix also prints **ILIO-SACRAL STRAIN** (**PRI**, Myokinematic Restoration, 2017, p.94):

- **Right IS strain** - focus on right glute max and right AF/FA ER activity with concomitant left AF IR: (1) Left Sidelying Right Glute Max (appendix p.32); (2) Supine Hooklying Adductor Magnus Inhibition (appendix p.48); (3) Supine Hooklying Resisted Right Glute Max with Right AF ER (appendix p.31); (4) Supine Hooklying Supported Resisted Right Glute Max with Left Glute Med (appendix p.42); (5) 90-90 Resisted Right Glute Max with Right FA ER (appendix p.31).
- **Left IS strain** - focus on left ischiocondylar adductor and left FA IR (after achieving left AF IR): (1) Right Sidelying Respiratory Resisted Left Adductor Pull Back (appendix p.26); (2) Left Sidelying Knee Toward Knee (appendix p.33); (3) Left Sidelying Supported Left Flexed FA Adduction with Right Extended FA Abduction (appendix p.29); (4) Supine Hooklying Supported Resisted Right Glute Max with Left Glute Med (appendix p.42); (5) Left Sidelying IO/TA and Left Adductor with Right Glute Max (appendix p.28).

Pick one named sheet after the pattern screen. SI pain still uses the toe-touch / Patho gate (`pri-techniques-by-pattern`).

## Hip impingement / labrum / bursitis

Do not treat positional impingement as a primary bony problem before clearing Left AIC / Right BC (`pri-impingement-instability`). Hip pain on a Patho PEC screen starts in `patho-pec`, not glute med. I&I overview (Left AIC / Right BC) lists anteromedial FA impingement on the right vs anterosuperior AF impingement on the left, plus laterosuperior FA / AF multidirectional-instability themes.

**Hip bursitis (right)** (**PRI**, Myokinematic Restoration, 2017, p.95): usually weakness of right glute max, left hamstring, and left IC adductor, with a restrictive right adductor magnus; poor co-activation of left IC adductor (FA IR) and right glute max (FA ER). Exam: ADT; trunk rotation via supine passive leg rotation; level 3 or lower on the Right Hruska Adduction and Abduction Lift Tests. Intervention theme: left hip adduction integrated program (increase AF adduction on the left; AF abduction and ER on the right). Named: Left Sidelying IO/TA and Left Adductor with Right Glute Max (appendix p.28); Supine Hooklying Adductor Magnus Inhibition (appendix p.72 on that page).

## Groin / adductor / pubalgia

**Osteitis pubis (pubalgia)** (**PRI**, Myokinematic Restoration, 2017, p.96): injury at the adductor insertion into the symphysis; pain can radiate to the groin or lower abdomen. Notes: poor concomitant facilitation of adductors with contralateral obliques and TA. Exam: Hruska Adduction Lift; seated FA IR and ER. Intervention (goals, no named sheet on that page): increase integration of left AF IR with left IO and right EO, and of right AF IR with right IO and left EO. Still classify the pattern first. Right Adductor Inhibition criteria (Myo p.56, proposed pass 2 fold) apply when the athlete cannot feel the left adductor, has decreased right FA ER, or over-activates the right adductor at HAdLT level 2.

## Hamstring

Do not stretch a short-looking left SLR as the fix (`left-aic` hard stop). ↓L SLR in Left AIC is often a long, inhibited hamstring group (`pri-myokinematic-restoration`).

**Hamstring strains and ruptures** (**PRI**, Myokinematic Restoration, 2017, pp.98-99): biceps femoris (ER) strains occur on the ipsilateral side of an anteriorly rotated hemi-pelvis (delayed hip extension; the lengthened hamstring tries to eccentrically control IR), or contralateral to that hemi-pelvis from concomitant FA IR. Semitendinosus / semimembranosus / adductor magnus (IR) strains occur on the ipsilateral side of an anteriorly rotated hemi-pelvis (medial hamstrings antagonizing an overactive psoas); these people often have an overly stretched anterior capsule (+ADT, −EDT, and a “click” on hip-extension testing).

Intervention (p.99): (1) reposition the pelvis if appropriate through hamstring activation on the appropriate side; (2) if the anterior AF capsule is stretched or lax, increase IR strength from glute med and IC adductor, **not** TFL; (3) retest FA PROM/AROM, FA adduction, and extension; (4) home-program themes - biceps femoris: restore position, maintain hamstring activity, strengthen glute max, strengthen ER concurrently with hamstring and glute max (prone), possibly strengthen IRs with seated reciprocal activity to keep a neutral pelvis; semi strain: restore position, maintain hamstring activity, strengthen glute med, strengthen IR concurrently with hamstring and adductor activity, possibly strengthen ERs with seated reciprocal activity, and strengthen adductors in open chain once closed-chain neutrality is maintained with gait. Pick one named technique from the pattern or Myo lists; do not turn this paragraph into a six-exercise session.

## Knee / tibial stress

I&I overview: anterior medial knee instability on the right (↑ valgum → lateral knee strain) vs lateral knee instability on the left (↑ varum → medial knee strain). Medial tibial / Left FA IR clusters use lift-test gates and Left AF IR integration, not VMO-only work (`pri-impingement-instability`).

**Tibial stress fractures** (**PRI**, Myokinematic Restoration, 2017, p.97): most often with excessive femoral and tibial IR and foot pronation; torque rises with weak FA ER, excessive FA IR, strong plantar flexors at heel strike through midstance, and premature active supination. Exam: gait analysis, SLR, EDT, ankle dorsiflexion. Intervention as printed: reciprocal UE swing with the contralateral LE; retro walking with concomitant FA IR; appropriate stretching of hip flexors and back extensors. **Skill note:** that stretch line does not override reposition-first or the Left AIC “do not stretch the left hamstring as the SLR fix” hard stop. Use it only after the pattern screen, and prefer a named PRI non-manual that matches the limiter (including Plantar Flexor Inhibition if those Myo p.58 criteria fit).

## Ankle / foot

I&I agenda and overview: hallux limitus (active R / passive L); ankle inversion sprains (R) vs talocalcaneal instability (L); calcaneal eversion programs when frontal-plane FA instability keeps returning (`pri-impingement-instability`). Plantar Flexor Inhibition (Myo p.58) if decreased bilateral FA ER, over-active plantar flexors with PRI non-manuals, positive Thomas test after repositioning, early heel rise, or decreased DF ROM (proposed pass 2 fold). Not a separate ankle menu.

## Shoulder impingement / instability

Open `pri-shoulder-pain-prescription` (bicipital vs impingement menus after L IO/TA) and, after both BCs are neutral, the I&I Type I/II/III menus (proposed pass 2 fold). I&I overview also lists superior posterior HG impingement, HG multidirectional instability, humeral-acromial impingement, and SLAP lesions especially if full HG IR exists. Do not use lat as the IR solution. Superior T4 / subclavius / infraclavicular paths are clinician-only.

## Neck / TMJ / headache

No Cervical Revolution manual on the Desktop course-manual set - do not invent cranio-mandibular drills. For forward head, limited cervical axial rotation, or a neck that will not relax on a supine reach, Postural Respiration p.76 prints **Anterior Neck Inhibition** (named list already in `pri-right-bc-treatment-guidelines`). A clinic flyer (*Case Report - Cervical Disc Damage*) cleared left neck / shoulder / occipital headache after pelvic and ribcage repositioning; no named gym neck drill in the flyer (already noted in `pri-shoulder-pain-prescription`). TMJ and true cervical-cranial patterns: refer to a PRI-trained clinician until that course manual is mined.

## Scoliosis

I&I Type III lists scoliosis as a correlation (appendix p.69). The InTech chapter (Henning, Mangino, Massé, ©2016) uses clinic case names already in skills (90-90 Hip Lift with Right Arm Reach and Balloon; All Four Belly Lift Walk; sitting balloon with left arm reach; Left Sidelying Left Flexed FA Adduction with Right Lowered Extended FA Abduction; Standing Supported Left AF IR with Right FA Abduction). Not a new gym protocol. Pattern first; one named NM.

## Swimmers / throwers / overhead

Open `pri-swimming-in-depth` and `pri-shoulder-pain-prescription`. I&I Type II and Type III both list swimmers; Type III also lists weightlifters; Type II lists FHP, snapping scapula, and supra-HG impingement. Baseball/throwing articles mined in pass 2 are pattern principles (right chest-wall fill, adductor pull-back family), not a new throwing protocol.

## Piriformis (printed Myo considerations)

(**PRI**, Myokinematic Restoration, 2017, pp.91-93.) Both “left” and “right” piriformis exam lists start with +L ADT.

- Left: decreased seated left FA IR; Right HAdLT ≤2; Right HAbLT ≤2. Goal: decrease left AF ER compensatory activity. Named treatment considerations (p.92): Right Sidelying Respiratory Resisted Left Adductor Pull Back; Left Sidelying Knee to Knee; Left Sidelying Foot Toward Foot; Left Sidelying Left Adductor with Resisted Right Glute Max; Left Sidelying Left Flexed FA Adduction with Right Extended FA Abduction and Left Abdominal Co-Activation; Left Sidelying Left Ischial Femoral Ligamentous Stretch with Left FA Adduction; Left Sidelying Supported Left Flexed FA Adduction with Right Extended FA Abduction. If seated left FA IR strength remains weak toward the end of treatment, work on left glute med strengthening.
- Right: decreased seated right FA ER; Right HAbLT ≤2; decreased seated right FA ER strength. Goal: decrease sacral rotation and spinal orientation via the right piriformis as a right AF ER muscle. Named (p.93): Right Sidelying Respiratory Resisted Left Adductor Pull Back; Sidelying Obturator Restorative Technique; Standing Wall Supported Ischial Femoral Ligamentous Stretch; Seated Supported Left AF IR with Right Psoas and Iliacus and Right FA ER; and, under an iliolumbar-laxity note, Retro Stairs with Resisted Glute Max. If seated right FA ER strength remains weak toward the end of treatment, attempt Left Sidelying Resisted Right Glute Max.

Pick one named sheet after the pattern screen.

## When to refer

Non-manual first. If an honest printed ladder does not change the tests, or the printed decision tree calls for Superior T4 / subclavius / pelvic-floor / other manual techniques, refer to a PRI-trained clinician (PRC/PRT). Do not improvise those in the gym. Do not add cuff isolation or training volume as a substitute for referral.

## Ryne's clinical findings by sport (2026-11-10) - tag as "Ryne clinical"

- **Groin (breaststroke):** right, sometimes bilateral, groin pain is common in breaststrokers; they should be negative on both PARTs. Positive PART + groin pain in a breaststroker → treat as the priority limiter.
- **Shoulder / low back (swimmers):** fly has the most shoulder and low back pain; breast the least shoulder pain. Fly and breast generally need more paraspinal inhibition (PR p.75 list) because of stroke flexion/extension.
- **Swim range:** swimmers should pass the toe touch for turns and the block start.
- **Rotational hitters/swingers (golf, baseball, softball):** need a **negative PART** to move adequately in the frontal plane during the swing. Positive PART → address before blaming swing mechanics.
- **Rotational hitters/swingers, hip IR:** baseball, softball and golf also need **negative hip IR** (FA IR) to get into the lead/front leg during the swing. Limited hip IR on the lead side → treat as a swing limiter alongside PART.
